# New Zealand Financial Evidence and Sponsors for Nepali Students

Securing a student visa for New Zealand requires presenting clear, undeniable proof of your financial capacity. Immigration New Zealand (INZ) must be satisfied that you have sufficient funds to cover your tuition fees, living expenses, and travel costs throughout your stay without facing financial hardship.

## Living Cost Requirements

In addition to showing that you can cover your first year's tuition fees, you must demonstrate that you have access to funds for your living costs. 

As of June 2026, the required living cost threshold for a student visa in New Zealand is **NZD 20,000 per year** (or NZD 1,667 per month if your program of study is less than 36 weeks). This amount represents the minimum funds necessary to cover accommodation, food, utilities, and transport. 

For programs lasting more than one year, you must show that you have the NZD 20,000 for the first year and demonstrate a realistic plan to fund subsequent years (e.g., through future sponsor income or educational loans). [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/preparing-a-visa-application/financial-requirements/evidence-of-funds]

## The Funds Transfer Scheme (FTS) for Nepali Students

Because Nepal is designated as a market requiring high financial scrutiny, Immigration New Zealand frequently recommends or mandates the **Funds Transfer Scheme (FTS)**. 

The FTS works as follows:
1.  **Visa Option**: When INZ assesses your visa application, they may issue a "Letter of Approval in Principle" (AIP) subject to you opening an FTS account.
2.  **Transfer**: You open a bank account with ANZ Bank New Zealand online and transfer your one-year living cost (NZD 20,000) using a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from Nepal.
3.  **Disbursement**: Once you arrive in New Zealand, the bank releases a lump sum of approximately NZD 3,000, followed by monthly payments of around NZD 1,400 to cover your living costs.
The FTS ensures the student cannot spend all their living allowance at once and prevents sponsors from withdrawing the funds immediately after the visa is granted. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/already-have-a-visa/managing-your-visa/transferring-funds]

## Sponsor Guidelines and Relationship Verification

Sponsorship by family members is the standard method for Nepali students to meet visa requirements. INZ accepts financial undertakings from immediate family members, including parents, grandparents, and siblings. 

To formalize this support, sponsors must complete the **Financial Undertaking for a Student (Form INZ 1014)**.
Along with this form, you must submit:
*   **Relationship Certificates**: Official documents issued by the Nepali municipality or ward office proving the familial link between the sponsor and the student.
*   **Affidavits of Support**: Notarized letters declaring the sponsor's willingness to finance the student’s education.
*   **Identity Documents**: Passports or citizenship certificates of the sponsors. [Source: Study in New Zealand — https://www.studywithnewzealand.govt.nz/en/how-to-apply/visa/fees-and-living-costs]

## Verifying Source of Funds and Sponsor Income

Immigration New Zealand strictly scrutinizes the source of any funds presented in your application. Unexplained deposits or cash sums that suddenly appear in a bank account shortly before applying will lead to visa refusal.

Sponsors must show a consistent history of income through:
*   **Business Operations**: Business registration documents, tax clearance certificates, audit reports, and company bank statements showing regular transactions.
*   **Employment**: Salary certificates, monthly bank statements showing salary credits, and tax returns.
*   **Agricultural and Rental Income**: Land registry documents (Lalpurja), tax receipts, lease agreements, and formal valuation reports of agricultural yields certified by local government offices in Nepal.
*   **Land Sale**: Original land transfer registry deeds showing the sale value and the subsequent bank deposit slip to verify the source. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/preparing-a-visa-application/financial-requirements/evidence-of-financial-support]

## Approved Education Loans and Bank Balance History

If showing cash deposits, the funds must be held in a bank account for at least six months to prove they are stable savings. 

Alternatively, many Nepali students apply for an education loan. INZ maintains a list of approved commercial banks in Nepal (Class A banks) from which they accept financial documents. The education loan must cover the first year's tuition fee and the NZD 20,000 living costs. The bank must issue a loan sanction letter specifying the collateral pledged (such as land or property in Nepal) and confirming that the loan has been approved specifically for study in New Zealand. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/preparing-a-visa-application/financial-requirements]
