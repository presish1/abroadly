# New Zealand Post-Study Work and Long-Term Options for Nepali Students

For many Nepali students, studying in New Zealand is a stepping stone to global career opportunities and potential permanent residency. Understanding the rules governing post-study work rights and the immigration pathways available for skilled graduates is essential for making informed long-term plans.

## The Post-Study Work Visa (PSWV)

The Post-Study Work Visa (PSWV) allows international graduates to work for any employer in New Zealand. The duration of the PSWV depends directly on the level of qualification completed under the New Zealand Qualifications Framework (NZQF):

*   **Master’s Degrees (Level 9) and PhDs (Level 10)**: Eligible for a **3-year** open post-study work visa, regardless of the duration of their studies.
*   **Bachelor’s Degrees (Level 7) and Postgraduate Diplomas (Level 8)**: Eligible for a PSWV that matches the duration of their study (typically **3 years** for a Bachelor's and **1 year** for a PG Diploma).
*   **Graduate Diplomas (Level 7) and Diplomas (Levels 5 and 6)**: Eligible for a PSWV only if the qualification is on the Green List or matches an eligible trade occupation. The visa duration is typically **1 year** and the work must align with the field of study.

As of June 2026, students must ensure their specific course of study is registered on the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students (CRICOS equivalent in NZ - NZQA) and satisfies PSWV requirements before enrolling. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/already-have-a-visa/managing-your-visa/post-study-work-visa]

## The Green List and In-Demand Pathways

Immigration New Zealand operates a **Green List** of high-skilled, in-demand roles that have expedited pathways to residence. The list is designed to address critical labor shortages:

*   **Tier 1: Straight to Residence**: Graduates who secure a job offer in a Tier 1 occupation can apply for residency immediately. This includes roles such as civil engineers, software engineers, doctors, and ICT project managers.
*   **Tier 2: Work to Residence**: Graduates who secure a job offer in a Tier 2 occupation can apply for residency after working in that role in New Zealand for two years. This includes roles such as secondary school teachers, registered nurses, and specialized trade workers.

Selecting a program of study that aligns with Green List roles is a popular strategy for Nepali students aiming for residency. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/preparing-a-visa-application/working-in-nz/green-list-roles]

## Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) Resident Visa

For graduates whose occupations are not on the Green List, the primary pathway to residence is the points-based **Skilled Migrant Category (SMC)**. New Zealand uses a simplified **6-point system** for SMC:

To apply, you must secure a skilled job offer from an Accredited Employer in New Zealand, meet the English language requirements, and accumulate at least 6 points. Points can be claimed from one of the following pillars:
1.  **Qualifications**: 3 points for a Bachelor’s degree, 4 points for a Bachelor’s with Honours or PG Diploma, 5 points for a Master’s degree, and 6 points for a PhD.
2.  **Professional Registration**: Points based on years of registration in New Zealand for designated occupations (like doctors, lawyers, or nurses).
3.  **Income**: 3 to 6 points if your salary is at least 1.5 to 2 times the New Zealand median wage.
Additionally, you can claim 1 point per year of skilled work experience in New Zealand, up to a maximum of 3 points, to combine with your qualification or registration points to reach the required 6-point target. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/explore-visa-options/work/resident-visas/skilled-migrant-category-resident-visa]

## Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV)

If your PSWV is expiring and you do not yet qualify for residency, you can transition to an **Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV)**. 

To obtain an AEWV:
*   You must have a job offer from a New Zealand employer who is accredited with INZ.
*   The job must pay at least the median wage (unless exempt under specific sector agreements).
*   The employer must conduct a Job Check to prove they have advertised the role locally and were unable to find a suitable New Zealand citizen or resident. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/explore-visa-options/work/temporary-work-visas/accredited-employer-work-visa]
