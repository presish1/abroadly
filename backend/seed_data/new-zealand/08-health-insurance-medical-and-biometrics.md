# New Zealand Health Insurance, Medicals, and Biometrics for Nepali Students

To enter and study in New Zealand, international students must meet strict health, character, and identity requirements. Immigration New Zealand (INZ) uses these measures to protect public health and manage healthcare resources. Additionally, New Zealand law mandates that all international students hold comprehensive health insurance throughout their study period.

## Medical Certificates and Chest X-Rays in Nepal

Health requirements vary depending on the length of your planned stay in New Zealand:

*   **Stays between 6 and 12 months**: Because Nepal is identified as a country with a high incidence of tuberculosis (TB), you must submit a **Chest X-ray Certificate (Form INZ 1096)**.
*   **Stays longer than 12 months**: You must provide both a **Chest X-ray Certificate** and a **General Medical Certificate (Form INZ 1007)**.

All medical examinations must be completed by an INZ-approved panel physician. In Kathmandu, Nepal, the approved panel clinics are:
1.  **International Organization for Migration (IOM)**: Lazimpat, Kathmandu.
2.  **Norvic International Hospital**: Thapathali, Kathmandu.

The panel physician conducts physical exams, urine and blood tests, and chest X-rays. The results are submitted electronically to INZ via the eMedical system. As of June 2026, these medical certificates are valid for up to 36 months from the date of issuance, provided your health status has not changed. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/preparing-a-visa-application/medical-info/getting-a-medical-exam]

## Biometrics Collection at VFS Global

Nepali students must submit their biometrics as part of the student visa application process. After submitting your visa online, INZ will issue a Biometrics Collection Letter.

You must schedule an appointment at the VFS Global New Zealand Visa Application Centre (VAC) in Kathmandu, located at the Chhaya Center, Thamel. You must bring:
*   Your physical passport.
*   The Biometrics Collection Letter from INZ.
*   The VFS appointment booking receipt.

VFS staff will take a digital passport photo and scan your fingerprints. These details are matched with your online visa application. The biometrics submission must be completed within the timeframe specified in the INZ letter (usually 14 days) to avoid processing delays. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/preparing-a-visa-application/character-and-identity/biometrics]

## Compulsory Student Insurance and the Code of Practice

Under the Education (Pastoral Care of Tertiary and International Learners) Code of Practice, all international students in New Zealand must have compliant travel and medical insurance from the date they enroll until their student visa expires.

The insurance policy must meet specific standards set by the New Zealand Qualifications Authority (NZQA), covering:
*   General medical treatment, GP consultations, and prescription medications.
*   Emergency evacuation and repatriation of remains.
*   Travel insurance for your journey to and from New Zealand.

Most universities and Private Training Establishments (PTEs) automatically enroll students in a pre-approved policy (such as Southern Cross "StudentSafe" or Allianz Care) and add the fee (typically NZD 700 to NZD 900 per year) to the tuition invoice. If you choose to buy your own insurance from a provider in Nepal or elsewhere, it must be officially approved by your institution before they will permit you to register for classes. [Source: NZQA — https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/providers-partners/tertiary-and-international-learners-code/]

## Accessing Healthcare Services in New Zealand

New Zealand has a high-quality healthcare system. For non-emergency medical issues, students should visit a **General Practitioner (GP)**. 

*   **Campus Clinics**: Most universities and large polytechnics have on-campus student health centers. Consultations here are highly subsidized or free for students covered by the university's default insurance.
*   **Pharmacies**: Pharmacists can provide advice and over-the-counter medicine for minor ailments like colds or minor skin irritations.
*   **Emergencies**: For life-threatening emergencies, call **111** for an ambulance, or go directly to the nearest hospital Emergency Department. 
Always carry your student ID and insurance policy details when visiting any medical professional. [Source: Study in New Zealand — https://www.studywithnewzealand.govt.nz/en/live/health-and-wellbeing]
