# New Zealand Application Timeline and FAQ for Nepali Students

Careful timing and a thorough understanding of New Zealand's immigration processes are vital to securing a student visa. Nepali applicants must prepare for admission, financial verification, and potential interviews well in advance to align with New Zealand’s academic intakes.

## Recommended Application Timeline

New Zealand has two main academic intakes: Semester 1 (February - the primary intake) and Semester 2 (July - mid-year intake). Below is a monthly guide tailored for the February intake:

*   **July to August (Test Preparation & Academic Assembly)**:
    *   Research universities, Te Pūkenga (polytechnics), and Private Training Establishments (PTEs).
    *   Take English proficiency tests (IELTS or PTE Academic).
    *   Collect and scan all academic transcripts, certificates, and reference letters.
*   **September to October (Applications & Offers)**:
    *   Apply to your selected New Zealand education providers. 
    *   Receive an "Offer of Place" (unconditional or conditional).
    *   If conditional, submit outstanding documents (e.g., final test scores) to secure an unconditional Offer of Place.
*   **October to November (Financial Assembly & Visa Lodgement)**:
    *   Gather financial sponsorship documents, bank balance histories (held for 6 months), or secure an education loan from an approved Nepali bank.
    *   Draft a comprehensive Statement of Purpose (SOP).
    *   Submit your student visa application online through the RealMe portal.
*   **November to December (Medicals, Biometrics, & Interviews)**:
    *   Complete your chest X-ray and medical examination at an approved panel physician in Kathmandu.
    *   Submit biometrics at VFS Global in Kathmandu.
    *   Prepare for the INZ telephone interview.
*   **December to January (AIP & Payment Transfer)**:
    *   Receive your Approval in Principle (AIP) letter from INZ.
    *   Apply for the No Objection Certificate (NOC) in Nepal.
    *   Pay tuition fees to the institution and transfer living costs (NZD 20,000) to your FTS account.
    *   Upload receipts to the RealMe portal.
*   **January to February (Visa Grant & Travel)**:
    *   Receive your eVisa.
    *   Book flights, arrange student insurance, find accommodation, and travel to New Zealand. [Source: Study in New Zealand — https://www.studywithnewzealand.govt.nz/en/how-to-apply/visa/prepare-for-your-visa-application]

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) for Nepali Students

### Q: Can I apply for a New Zealand student visa if I have a study gap?
Yes. A study gap is acceptable if it is backed by genuine activity. You must provide official proof, such as employment contracts, salary slips, experience letters, or training certificates. Gaps of several years that are unexplained or unsupported by documentation will lead to a visa refusal under the genuine student rule.

### Q: What is an "Offer of Place" and how is it different from a Letter of Acceptance?
In New Zealand, the document confirming your admission is called an **Offer of Place**. It is issued by the education provider and includes details like the course title, NZQF level, start and end dates, tuition fees, and whether the course meets NZQA standards. You cannot apply for a student visa without a valid Offer of Place.

### Q: What happens if I fail my INZ telephone interview?
If you are unable to answer questions about your course modules, the career opportunities in Nepal, or details of your sponsors’ finances, the immigration officer will conclude that you are not a genuine student. This is one of the most common reasons for visa refusal. Be sure to study your SOP and course syllabus thoroughly before your interview.

### Q: Can I change my program or school after arriving in New Zealand?
Yes, but you must apply for a **Variation of Conditions (VoC)** on your student visa. You are legally required to study the specific course at the specific institution printed on your visa. Studying at a different school or program without an approved VoC is a breach of visa conditions and can lead to deportation.

As of June 2026, staying informed about immigration updates ensures a compliant and stress-free student journey in New Zealand. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/already-have-a-visa/managing-your-visa/variation-of-conditions-temporary-visas]
