# Fees, Scholarships, and Budgeting in New Zealand for Nepali Students

Studying in New Zealand requires careful financial planning. As an international student, you must pay full tuition fees, which are not subsidized by the New Zealand government. You also need to prove you have sufficient funds to cover your living costs.

## Tuition Fees

Tuition fees vary significantly depending on the institution (University vs. Te Pūkenga vs. PTE) and the field of study.

*   **Undergraduate Degrees (Bachelor's)**: Expect to pay between NZD 22,000 and NZD 35,000 per year. Arts and humanities tend to be on the lower end, while engineering, science, and specialized fields are higher. Medical and veterinary sciences can exceed NZD 75,000 per year [Source: Education New Zealand — https://www.studywithnewzealand.govt.nz/].
*   **Postgraduate Degrees (Master's/PhD)**: Master's degrees typically range from NZD 26,000 to NZD 40,000+ per year.
    *   *Important Exception for PhDs*: International PhD students studying in New Zealand pay the exact same tuition fees as domestic New Zealand students (often around NZD 7,000 - 9,000 per year). This makes New Zealand a highly attractive destination for doctoral research.
*   **Diplomas and Certificates**: Programs at Te Pūkenga or PTEs are generally more affordable, often ranging from NZD 18,000 to NZD 25,000 per year.

*Note: Fees are indicative as of June 2026. Always check the specific institution's website for exact costs.*

## Living Costs

Immigration New Zealand has a strict minimum financial requirement to grant a student visa.

*   **Minimum Requirement**: As of June 2026, you must prove you have at least **NZD 20,000 per year** for living expenses, in addition to your tuition fees and return airfare. If your course is less than a year, you need NZD 1,667 for each month of study [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/].
*   **Realistic Budget**: While NZD 20,000 is the minimum for the visa, actual living costs in major cities like Auckland or Wellington are often higher. A more realistic budget is NZD 22,000 to NZD 26,000 per year to cover accommodation (renting a room in a flatshare or homestay), food, transport, electricity, internet, and personal expenses.

## Scholarships

Scholarships in New Zealand for international undergraduate students are highly competitive and generally only offer partial tuition fee discounts (e.g., NZD 5,000 - 10,000 for the first year). Full scholarships at the bachelor's level are extremely rare.

*   **Manaaki New Zealand Scholarships**: These are fully funded government scholarships, but they are primarily targeted at specific developing countries and often focus on postgraduate study in specific sectors (like renewable energy or agriculture). Nepali students should check the current eligible country list on the MFAT website to see if Nepal is currently included in the bilateral program [Source: New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (MFAT) — https://www.nzscholarships.govt.nz/].
*   **University-Specific Scholarships**: All eight universities offer various scholarships for international students based on academic merit. Check the "International Scholarships" section of your chosen university's website.
*   **Postgraduate Research Scholarships**: There is more funding available at the Master's (by research) and PhD levels through university stipends or specific research grants.

## Budgeting Advice for Nepali Students

1.  **Do Not Rely on Part-Time Work**: While you can work part-time, Immigration New Zealand requires you to have your living funds secured *before* you arrive. You cannot use projected income from a part-time job in New Zealand to satisfy the visa financial requirements.
2.  **Bank Loans (Education Loans)**: Many Nepali students use education loans from "A" class commercial banks in Nepal (like Nabil, NIC Asia, SBI, Standard Chartered, etc.) to show financial capacity. Ensure the loan clearly covers the required tuition and living costs.
3.  **Calculate the Total Cost**: Multiply the annual tuition and living costs by the total number of years of your degree (e.g., 3 years for a bachelor's) to understand the full financial commitment.
