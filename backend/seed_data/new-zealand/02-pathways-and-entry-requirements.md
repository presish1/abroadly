# New Zealand Pathways and Entry Requirements for Nepali Students

Navigating entry requirements for New Zealand institutions depends heavily on your current educational background and the type of program you wish to pursue. New Zealand offers various pathways, from direct entry into bachelor's programs to foundation years and postgraduate study.

## Pathways After Class 12

For Nepali students who have completed Class 12 (NEB), direct entry into a New Zealand bachelor's degree is possible if you meet the specific academic and English language requirements.

- **Direct Entry (Bachelor's)**: Generally, a minimum of 65% to 75% in the NEB Class 12 examinations is required for direct entry into a university bachelor's program, depending on the university and the specific course. Highly competitive courses like engineering or health sciences may require higher scores [Source: Education New Zealand — https://www.studywithnewzealand.govt.nz/].
- **Foundation Programs**: If your Class 12 scores do not meet the direct entry requirements, you can enroll in a Foundation Certificate or Diploma program. These typically last 8 to 12 months and, upon successful completion, guarantee entry into the first year of a bachelor's degree at a partnered university.
- **Institutes of Technology and Polytechnics (ITPs)**: ITPs (now part of Te Pūkenga) often have slightly more flexible entry requirements for diplomas and bachelor's degrees compared to universities, focusing more on applied learning.

## Postgraduate Pathways

For students who have completed a bachelor's degree in Nepal, New Zealand offers several postgraduate options.

- **Master's Degree**: Direct entry into a master's program usually requires a relevant bachelor's degree with a good academic record (typically equivalent to a B grade average or higher in New Zealand). Some master's programs, especially those requiring professional registration, may have additional requirements.
- **Postgraduate Diploma (PGDip)**: A PGDip is a one-year program that often serves as the first year of a two-year master's degree or as a standalone qualification. It requires a relevant bachelor's degree for entry.
- **Graduate Diploma (GradDip)**: If you want to study a subject different from your undergraduate degree, a Graduate Diploma is often the best pathway. It is a Level 7 qualification (same level as a bachelor's degree) that typically takes one year and allows you to transition into a new field or progress to a master's degree in that new field.

## English Language Requirements

All international students from non-English speaking backgrounds must prove their English proficiency. The requirements vary by the level of study and the specific institution.

- **Undergraduate (Bachelor's)**: The standard requirement is an IELTS overall score of 6.0, with no band less than 5.5. Equivalent scores in TOEFL iBT (usually around 80) or PTE Academic (usually around 50) are also widely accepted.
- **Postgraduate (Master's/PGDip)**: The standard requirement is an IELTS overall score of 6.5, with no band less than 6.0. Some programs, particularly in teaching, nursing, or law, may require an IELTS of 7.0 or higher.
- **Foundation/Diploma Programs**: These often require a lower IELTS score, typically around 5.5 overall.

*Note: Always check the specific requirements for your chosen program, as they can change.* [Source: NZQA — https://www.nzqa.govt.nz/]

## Credential Recognition (NZQA)

The New Zealand Qualifications Authority (NZQA) assesses international qualifications. While most universities have their own internal assessment processes for standard Nepali degrees (like a 4-year bachelor's from Tribhuvan University or Kathmandu University), you may occasionally need an International Qualifications Assessment (IQA) from NZQA if your qualification is less common or if you are applying for specific professional registration or a skilled migrant visa later on.
