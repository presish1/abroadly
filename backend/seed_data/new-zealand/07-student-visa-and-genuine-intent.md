# New Zealand Student Visa and Genuine Intent for Nepali Students

Navigating the student visa process for New Zealand requires understanding the specific pathways offered and satisfying the strict "genuine student" criteria. Unlike many other study destinations, New Zealand utilizes a two-stage visa approval process and frequently conducts telephone interviews to verify applicant intentions.

## Student Visa Pathways and the RealMe Portal

Nepali students typically apply for one of two main student visa types online via the official **RealMe portal**:

1.  **Fee Paying Student Visa**: The most common visa, allowing you to study a full-time course for up to four years at an approved school, university, or private training establishment (PTE).
2.  **Pathway Student Visa**: Allows you to study up to three consecutive courses on a single student visa for up to five years. For example, you can combine an English language program, a foundation certificate, and a bachelor's degree under one visa, saving time and application fees.

As of June 2026, the application fee for a student visa is NZD 750. The application must be accompanied by a Letter of Offer from a New Zealand Qualifications Authority (NZQA) accredited institution, stating the course details, duration, and tuition fees. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/explore-visa-options/study]

## The Approval in Principle (AIP) Process

A unique feature of the New Zealand student visa process is the **Approval in Principle (AIP)**.

When you submit your application, you do not pay your tuition fees upfront. Instead, you provide your financial documentation showing that the funds are available. 
*   **Assessment**: The immigration officer reviews your academic records, financial proof, and genuine intent.
*   **AIP Issuance**: If satisfied, the officer issues a letter stating your application has been Approved in Principle.
*   **Fee Payment**: You then use this AIP letter to obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC) in Nepal, wire your tuition fees to the institution, and (if required) transfer your living costs to your ANZ bank account under the Funds Transfer Scheme (FTS).
*   **Final Visa**: Once the institution and the bank confirm receipt of these funds, you upload the receipts, and INZ issues your actual student visa. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/already-have-a-visa/managing-your-visa/paying-tuition-fees]

## The Genuine Student Requirement

Immigration New Zealand assesses every student visa application under the **genuine student** rule. The immigration officer must be satisfied that your primary purpose in New Zealand is study, that you have the academic capability to complete the program, and that you intend to return to Nepal or comply with visa terms upon graduation.

INZ will evaluate:
*   Your academic and work history, assessing if the chosen program represents a logical progression.
*   Your English language skills, ensuring they meet the requirements of both the course and the visa.
*   Your ties to Nepal, such as family, properties, or employment prospects, which would motivate you to return. [Source: Immigration New Zealand — https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/preparing-a-visa-application/character-and-identity/genuine-student-intent]

## The Statement of Purpose (SOP) and INZ Interview

The Statement of Purpose (SOP) is a vital written document that outlines your reasons for studying in New Zealand. 

In addition to reviewing your SOP, INZ officers frequently conduct **telephone interviews** with Nepali applicants. These interviews are highly detailed and test the applicant’s knowledge in several areas:
*   **Course Details**: The specific subjects you will study, the credit requirements, the campus location, and how the program is assessed (exams vs. coursework).
*   **Cost and Funding**: The exact tuition fees, living costs, and detail about your sponsors’ income sources.
*   **Career Prospects**: The specific job titles and companies in Nepal where you plan to apply after graduation, and how the New Zealand qualification gives you an advantage over local graduates.
Any contradictions between the information in your written SOP and your verbal answers during the telephone interview will raise concerns regarding your credibility and can result in visa refusal. [Source: Study in New Zealand — https://www.studywithnewzealand.govt.nz/en/how-to-apply/visa/prepare-for-your-visa-application]
