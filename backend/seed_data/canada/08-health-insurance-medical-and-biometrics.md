# Canada Health Insurance, Medicals, and Biometrics for Nepali Students

Preparing for studies in Canada involves completing several administrative and health-related milestones. Before arriving and during your stay, you must satisfy strict health and biometrics requirements set by the Canadian government. Understanding these processes ensures a smooth visa application and protects your well-being while living abroad.

## Upfront Medical Examinations in Nepal

To expedite the study permit application, Nepali students are highly encouraged to undergo an "upfront medical exam." This means scheduling and completing your medical checkup before submitting your visa application, allowing you to upload the eMedical information sheet as part of your application.

The medical examination must be performed by an IRCC-approved panel physician. In Nepal, the authorized centers are:
*   **International Organization for Migration (IOM)**: Migration Health Assessment Centre located in Lazimpat, Kathmandu.
*   **Norvic International Hospital**: Located in Thapathali, Kathmandu.

During the exam, panel physicians check for general physical health, conduct blood and urine tests, and perform a chest X-ray to screen for active tuberculosis (TB). Once completed, the physician submits the results electronically to IRCC. The eMedical sheet provided to you serves as proof of completion for your visa application. As of June 2026, the medical certificate is valid for 12 months from the date of the exam. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/medical-exams/vistors-students-workers.html]

## Biometrics Appointment at VFS Global

Biometrics collection is a mandatory step for almost all Nepali study permit applicants. After submitting your online study permit application and paying the biometrics fee (CAD 85), IRCC will issue a Biometrics Instruction Letter (BIL).

With the BIL, you must book an appointment at the VFS Global Canada Visa Application Centre (VAC) in Kathmandu, located in Thamel. You must bring your valid passport, the appointment confirmation letter, and the BIL. At the VAC, staff will collect your digital fingerprints and a facial photograph. This information is securely transmitted to IRCC to verify your identity. Biometrics are valid for 10 years, meaning you do not need to repeat the process if you apply for another Canadian visa during this period. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/biometrics.html]

## Health Insurance Systems by Province

Canada does not have a single, unified national health insurance plan for international students. Instead, healthcare is managed by individual provinces and territories, and coverage policies for international students vary significantly:

*   **Ontario**: International students are not eligible for the provincial health plan (OHIP). Instead, universities and colleges require students to enroll in private plans, such as the University Health Insurance Plan (UHIP) or similar college insurance. The premium is typically billed automatically as part of your student fees.
*   **British Columbia**: International students staying for more than six months must enroll in the Medical Services Plan (MSP). There is a monthly health fee (approximately CAD 75) that students must pay directly to the provincial government.
*   **Alberta**: Students registered in a full-time program lasting 12 months or more may be eligible for free coverage under the Alberta Health Care Insurance Plan (AHCIP), though supplementary private coverage is still recommended for dental and vision care.

Before departing Nepal, you must verify how health insurance is handled by your specific DLI and ensure you have coverage starting from the day you land in Canada. [Source: EduCanada — https://www.educanada.ca/study-in-canada-etudes-au-canada/prepare-preparer/insurance-assurances.aspx?lang=eng]

## Maintaining Well-being and Accessing Care

Adjusting to Canada's climate, particularly the long winters, can pose physical and mental health challenges for students from Nepal. It is common to experience Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD) due to reduced sunlight. 

To manage your health:
*   **On-Campus Clinics**: Most Canadian universities and colleges have student wellness centers offering primary medical care, counseling services, and mental health support.
*   **Walk-in Clinics**: For non-emergency medical issues, you can visit local walk-in clinics without an appointment.
*   **Emergency Rooms**: In case of life-threatening emergencies, visit a hospital emergency department or call 911. 
Always carry a digital copy of your health insurance card and keep records of any existing medical conditions or prescription medications translated into English. [Source: EduCanada — https://www.educanada.ca/study-in-canada-etudes-au-canada/prepare-preparer/safety-securite.aspx?lang=eng]
