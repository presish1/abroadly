# Fees, Scholarships, and Budgeting in Canada for Nepali Students

Canada is generally considered more affordable than the US or the UK, but costs can still be high, especially in major cities like Toronto and Vancouver. Proper financial planning is crucial for both your visa application and your survival as a student.

## Tuition Fees

Tuition fees vary widely based on the province, the institution (college vs. university), and the program of study.

*   **Public Colleges (Diplomas/Certificates)**: This is the most affordable route. Tuition generally ranges from **CAD 15,000 to CAD 20,000 per year**.
*   **Undergraduate Universities (Bachelor's)**: Tuition is higher, typically ranging from **CAD 20,000 to CAD 40,000+ per year**. Highly ranked universities (like UofT or UBC) or specialized programs (engineering, medicine) can cost CAD 50,000 to CAD 60,000+ annually.
*   **Postgraduate Universities (Master's)**: Tuition generally ranges from **CAD 18,000 to CAD 30,000+ per year**. Executive programs or MBAs can be significantly higher.

*Note: These are indicative estimates as of June 2026. Always check the specific institution's website for exact and current fees.* [Source: EduCanada — https://www.educanada.ca/]

## Living Costs and the GIC

To get a study permit, IRCC requires you to prove you can support yourself financially.

*   **The Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC)**: To prove you have sufficient living expenses for your first year, IRCC strongly recommends (and requires for the SDS visa route) that you purchase a GIC from a participating Canadian bank.
*   **Current GIC Amount**: As of 2024, the required GIC amount was raised significantly to **CAD 20,635** for a single student (this amount is updated annually based on Statistics Canada's low-income cut-off; always verify the current amount for the year you are applying). This money is locked and disbursed to you in monthly installments over your first year in Canada to cover your rent, food, and living expenses. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents.html#doc3]
*   **Realistic Living Costs**: While the GIC covers the IRCC minimum, actual living costs in Toronto or Vancouver can easily exceed CAD 25,000 per year. Rent for a shared room can range from CAD 600 to CAD 1,200+ per month depending on the city.

## Scholarships

Scholarships for international students in Canada are highly competitive, especially at the undergraduate level.

*   **University Entrance Scholarships**: Many universities automatically consider you for entrance scholarships based on your high school (Class 12) grades. These are usually partial scholarships (e.g., CAD 2,000 - 10,000).
*   **College Scholarships**: Public colleges offer fewer and smaller scholarships, sometimes offering small bursaries (e.g., CAD 500 - 2,000) after you have completed your first semester with good grades.
*   **Fully Funded Scholarships**: Fully funded undergraduate scholarships are extremely rare. At the master's and PhD levels, more funding is available through research assistantships, teaching assistantships, and specific graduate awards (like the Vanier Canada Graduate Scholarships, though these are fiercely competitive).

## Budgeting Advice for Nepali Students

1.  **Factor in the GIC and First Year Tuition**: Before you even apply for your visa, you must have the liquid funds to pay your first year of tuition (e.g., CAD 18,000) PLUS the GIC amount (e.g., CAD 20,635). This means you need upwards of CAD 40,000 (~NPR 3.9 million) readily available just to start the process.
2.  **Education Loans**: Many Nepali students use education loans from "A" class commercial banks in Nepal to cover these massive upfront costs. Ensure your loan covers both tuition and the GIC transfer.
3.  **Part-Time Work is Supplementary**: You are allowed to work part-time (usually 20 hours/week off-campus). However, rely on this income only for supplementary living expenses (like groceries or phone bills), NOT to pay your next semester's tuition. Depending on part-time work to pay high tuition fees leads to extreme stress and often academic failure.
