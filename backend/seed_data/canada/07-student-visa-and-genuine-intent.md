# Canada Student Visa and Genuine Intent for Nepali Students

Obtaining a Letter of Acceptance (LOA) is only the beginning of your study journey. The most critical hurdle is securing a Canadian Study Permit. This process requires submitting a formal application to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), where you must demonstrate that you are a genuine student who intends to comply with visa regulations.

## The Study Permit Application Process

Nepali students generally submit their study permit applications online via the official IRCC Portal. The application consists of completing various family information and application forms, uploading all supporting documents, and paying the processing fees. As of June 2026, the study permit processing fee is CAD 150, and the biometrics fee is CAD 85. 

Once the application is submitted online, applicants receive a Biometrics Instruction Letter (BIL) within a few days. You must then schedule an appointment at the VFS Global office in Kathmandu, Nepal, to submit your fingerprints and photograph. The processing time for Nepali applications can vary from a few weeks to several months, making early submission crucial. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit.html]

## The Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) Requirement

A relatively recent and critical requirement in the study permit process is the Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL). Under rules designed to manage international student intake, most undergraduate-level and college-diploma applicants must submit a PAL alongside their LOA.

The PAL is issued by the provincial government (such as Ontario, British Columbia, or Alberta) where the institution is located. Students cannot apply directly to the province for a PAL; instead, their Designated Learning Institution (DLI) requests it from the provincial government on their behalf after the student pays their initial tuition deposit. Masters and doctoral degree applicants are exempt from this requirement, as are students applying for study permit extensions from within Canada. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents.html]

## The Genuine Student Requirement

Under section 216(1)(b) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR), a visa officer must be satisfied that the applicant will leave Canada at the end of their authorized stay. This is known as the "genuine student" assessment.

Visa officers examine the logical link between the student’s past education, work experience, and their chosen program in Canada. If a student with a Bachelor of Business Administration from Nepal applies for a college diploma in hospitality in Canada, the officer may question the progression of the academic pathway. The officer also evaluates whether the student has sufficient financial resources to prevent them from needing to work beyond the legally allowed limits. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare-arrival.html]

## Writing a Persuasive Statement of Purpose (SOP)

The Statement of Purpose (SOP) is your primary opportunity to speak directly to the visa officer. A well-written SOP is crucial for Nepali students, as face-to-face interviews are rarely conducted. 

Your SOP must cover:
*   **Academic Journey**: Your achievements, subject choices, and explanations for any gaps in your education.
*   **Program Rationale**: Why you chose this specific program and DLI, and why you are not pursuing a similar course in Nepal or neighboring countries.
*   **Career Goals**: Clear, realistic career plans in Nepal upon graduation, naming specific companies or sectors where you plan to work.
*   **Ties to Nepal**: Details of family relationships, properties, or businesses that necessitate your return. 
*   **Financial Feasibility**: A summary of how your tuition and living costs are funded.
Applicants must write their own SOP. The use of generic templates or obvious AI-generated text often leads to immediate visa refusal due to lack of authenticity. [Source: EduCanada — https://www.educanada.ca/study-in-canada-etudes-au-canada/apply-demande/visa.aspx?lang=eng]

## Documenting Study Gaps and Work Experience

Study gaps are common among Nepali applicants, often due to preparing for entrance exams, searching for jobs, or dealing with administrative delays. A study gap is not an automatic cause for refusal, provided it is well-documented.

If you have a gap, you must provide:
*   **Experience Letters**: Official employment letters showing your job title, responsibilities, and tenure.
*   **Salary Slips and Bank Statements**: Evidence showing that you were paid for your work, verifying the employment is genuine.
*   **Training and Certification**: Certificates from short courses or professional certifications completed during the gap.
Unexplained gaps of more than a year are heavily penalized by visa officers, who may conclude that the applicant is using the student visa as a pathway for economic migration rather than academic advancement. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents.html]
