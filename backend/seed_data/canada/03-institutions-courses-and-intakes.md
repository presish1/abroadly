# Institutions, Courses, and Intakes in Canada for Nepali Students

Canada's higher education system is decentralized, meaning it is managed at the provincial level rather than by the federal government. However, standards are consistently high across the country.

## Types of Institutions

There are two primary types of public post-secondary institutions in Canada:

1.  **Universities**: Focus on academic and professional degree programs (Bachelor's, Master's, PhDs). They are research-intensive.
    *   *Examples*: University of Toronto, University of British Columbia (UBC), McGill University, University of Waterloo.
2.  **Colleges (and Institutes of Technology / Polytechnics)**: Focus on applied, career-oriented programs (Certificates, Diplomas, Advanced Diplomas, Post-Graduate Certificates, and some Bachelor's degrees). They often feature smaller class sizes and mandatory co-op (internship) components.
    *   *Examples*: Seneca Polytechnic, Humber College, Centennial College, Langara College, Bow Valley College.

*Important Note on Private Colleges*: While there are many private colleges in Canada, **graduates from most private colleges are NOT eligible for a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)**. Always prioritize public universities and colleges, or ensure the specific private institution and program are explicitly eligible for the PGWP on the IRCC website.

## Designated Learning Institutions (DLI)

To apply for a Canadian study permit, you MUST be accepted by a **Designated Learning Institution (DLI)**. A DLI is a school approved by a provincial or territorial government to host international students. If your school is not on the DLI list, your visa application will be refused.

*   You must include the school's DLI number on your study permit application.
*   [Source: IRCC DLI List — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare/designated-learning-institutions-list.html]

## Popular Provinces for Nepali Students

1.  **Ontario**: The most popular province, home to Toronto and Ottawa. It has the highest concentration of universities and colleges, and a very large job market, but living costs are extremely high.
2.  **British Columbia (BC)**: Known for its mild climate (especially Vancouver) and beautiful scenery. Highly popular, but like Ontario, living costs and rent in Vancouver are among the highest in Canada.
3.  **Alberta**: Cities like Calgary and Edmonton offer a good balance of high-quality education, lower taxes (no provincial sales tax), and generally lower living costs compared to ON and BC. The economy is heavily tied to energy and tech.
4.  **Manitoba and Saskatchewan**: These prairie provinces are becoming increasingly popular due to lower tuition fees, lower living costs, and relatively faster Provincial Nominee Programs (PNP) for permanent residency.
5.  **Nova Scotia / Atlantic Provinces**: Offer a more relaxed lifestyle, lower costs, and specific immigration pathways (like the Atlantic Immigration Program).

## Intakes and Academic Calendar

Canada generally has three main intakes:

*   **Fall Intake (September)**: The primary intake. Almost all programs at universities and colleges accept applications for Fall. This is when the maximum number of scholarships are available.
*   **Winter Intake (January)**: The secondary intake. Many programs, especially at colleges and for some university master's degrees, offer a January start. It is a good option if you missed the Fall deadlines.
*   **Spring/Summer Intake (May)**: A minor intake. Very few programs start in May, mostly limited to specific short courses, language programs, or continuing education.
