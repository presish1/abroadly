# Canada Financial Evidence and Sponsors for Nepali Students

Demonstrating sufficient financial capacity is one of the most critical aspects of securing a Canadian study permit. The visa officer must be convinced that you can cover your tuition fees, living expenses, and return transportation without needing to work illegally. For Nepali applicants, providing a structured, well-documented financial profile is essential to show financial viability.

## The Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) Requirement

For international students, particularly those applying under the Student Direct Stream (SDS) or even standard routes, the Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) is a cornerstone of financial proof. As of June 2026, the required GIC amount is CAD 20,635 for a single applicant. This funds requirement represents the cost of living for one year in Canada, excluding tuition fees. 

Nepali students must purchase this GIC from an authorized Canadian financial institution, such as Scotiabank, CIBC, or SBI Canada Bank. The funds are wired from a Nepali bank using a No Objection Certificate (NOC) and a swift transfer. Once in Canada, the bank releases a portion of the GIC (approximately CAD 4,000) upon arrival, and the remaining amount is paid out in monthly installments over 12 months to cover living expenses. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/student-direct-stream.html]

## Tuition Fee Payment Proof

In addition to the GIC, applicants must prove they have paid their tuition fees. Under SDS, paying the full tuition fee for the first year of study is a mandatory requirement. You must submit a receipt from your Designated Learning Institution (DLI) confirming this payment. 

For non-SDS applications, while paying the first semester or full year's tuition is highly recommended, you can technically show proof of funds matching the first year's tuition in a bank account. However, visa officers in Nepal strongly favor applicants who have prepaid their first-year tuition, as it demonstrates genuine intent and reduces the likelihood of financial distress. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents.html]

## Eligible Sponsors and Relationship Certificates

Most Nepali students rely on family sponsors to fund their education. Eligible sponsors are typically immediate family members: parents, grandparents, and siblings. 

To establish the connection between the sponsor and the student, you must provide:
*   **Relationship Certificates**: Issued by the local ward or municipality office in Nepal, translated into English, and notarized.
*   **Sponsorship Letters**: An affidavit of support signed by the sponsors, stating their commitment to cover all academic and living expenses.
*   **Identification**: Copies of the sponsors' citizenship certificates or passports. [Source: EduCanada — https://www.educanada.ca/study-in-canada-etudes-au-canada/apply-demande/fees-frais.aspx?lang=eng]

## Demonstrating Source of Income

Simply showing a lump sum of money in a bank account is rarely sufficient. Visa officers will scrutinize the origin of the funds. Sponsors must provide credible evidence of their regular income.

Nepali sponsors typically present:
*   **Salary Income**: Salary certificates, bank statements showing salary deposits, and tax clearance certificates.
*   **Business Income**: Business registration documents, PAN/VAT certificates, audit reports, tax clearance certificates, and business bank statements.
*   **Land Lease and Agricultural Income**: Official lease agreements, land ownership certificates (Lalpurja), valuation reports, and agricultural sales receipts verified by local government bodies.
*   **Property Sales**: If funds were obtained by selling land, official land sale deeds (Rajinama) and tax receipts must be provided to explain the sudden influx of cash. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/prepare-arrival.html]

## Bank Balances and Education Loans

For non-SDS applicants or to show additional family savings, bank balance certificates are required. These certificates should show the current balance and be accompanied by bank statements spanning the last 4 to 6 months.

Alternatively, many Nepali students secure education loans. The loan must be from an 'A-class' commercial bank in Nepal approved by the Nepal Rastra Bank. The education loan certificate must state that the loan is specifically approved for the student's education in Canada. It must outline the collateral used and the disbursement terms. A clear paper trail showing the loan approval and partial disbursement is vital. [Source: IRCC — https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents.html]
