# Studying in Finland — Health Insurance, Medical, and Biometrics

Nepali students moving to Finland must comply with specific health insurance requirements and local administrative registration to ensure they have legal coverage and access to the Finnish healthcare system. As of June 2026, insurance policies must meet precise coverage minimums to be accepted by the immigration authorities [Source: Finnish Immigration Service — https://migri.fi].

## Mandatory Health Insurance

All international students must hold private health insurance for the entire duration of their residence permit.
- **Coverage Requirements:**
  - For studies lasting less than 2 years: The policy must cover medical expenses up to at least €120,000.
  - For studies lasting 2 years or more: The policy must cover medical expenses up to at least €40,000 (since the student has access to municipal healthcare after registration).
- **Approved Providers:** Swisscare, Student Insurance Program (SIP), and Aon Student Insurance are widely accepted.
- **Waiver Limitation:** The policy must not have a deductible (excess) exceeding €300.

## Finnish Student Health Service (FSHS / YTHS)

All degree students at Finnish universities and UAS are entitled to student healthcare services.
- **Healthcare Fee:** Students must pay a healthcare fee to Kela (the Social Insurance Institution of Finland) each term. As of 2026, this fee is approximately €36.80 per term [Source: Finnish Student Health Service — https://www.yths.fi/en].
- **Services Covered:** General health, mental health, and dental care.

## Local Registration (DVV) and Medical

- **Personal Identity Code:** Upon arrival, students must register at the Digital and Population Data Services Agency (DVV) to get a Finnish personal identity code (henkilötunnus) [Source: Digital and Population Data Services Agency — https://dvv.fi/en].
- **Tuberculosis Screening:** Students from countries with a high risk of tuberculosis (including Nepal) may be required to undergo a free TB chest X-ray screening at local health clinics in Finland.


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
- **Question:** Are there language proficiency exemptions available?
  **Answer:** Some universities offer English proficiency waivers if the student's previous medium of instruction was entirely in English. However, taking tests like IELTS, TOEFL, or Duolingo is strongly recommended to strengthen the student visa application and demonstrate academic readiness.
