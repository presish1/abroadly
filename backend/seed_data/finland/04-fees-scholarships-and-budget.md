# Studying in Finland — Fees, Scholarships, and Budgeting

Studying in Finland involves planning for tuition fees (for non-EU/EEA students) and living expenses. Fortunately, Finnish universities offer excellent scholarship opportunities to help offset costs. As of June 2026, understanding the financial obligations is a key requirement for securing admission and a residence permit [Source: Finnish National Agency for Education — https://www.studyinfo.fi].

## Tuition Fees for International Students

Tuition fees apply only to non-EU/EEA students studying in English-taught Bachelor's and Master's programs:
- **Bachelor's Programs:** €6,000 to €12,000 per academic year.
- **Master's Programs:** €8,000 to €18,000 per academic year.
- **Doctoral (PhD) Programs:** 100% tuition-free for all nationalities.

## Scholarship Opportunities

- **University Tuition Fee Waivers:** Most universities offer merit-based waivers covering 50% to 100% of the tuition fees for the second and subsequent years, conditional on completing at least 55 ECTS credits per year.
- **Finland Scholarships:** Funded by the Finnish government for Master's applicants. It covers 100% of the first-year tuition fee and provides a €5,000 relocation grant [Source: Ministry of Education and Culture — https://okm.fi/en].
- **Early Bird Discounts:** A discount of €1,000 to €2,000 on the first-year tuition fee if the student accepts the admission offer and pays within a specified timeframe (typically 14 days).

## Cost of Living and Student Benefits

The Finnish Immigration Service requires students to show they have at least **€800 per month (€9,600 per year)** in personal funds.
- **Student Housing:** Foundations like HOAS (Helsinki), TOAS (Tampere), or PSOAS (Oulu) offer subsidised student apartments. A single room in a shared apartment costs €250 to €450 per month.
- **Student Meal Subsidy:** Higher education students receive a state-subsidised warm lunch at university cafeterias for approximately €3.00.
- **Public Transport discounts:** 45% to 50% discount on city buses and trains.


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
- **Question:** Are there language proficiency exemptions available?
  **Answer:** Some universities offer English proficiency waivers if the student's previous medium of instruction was entirely in English. However, taking tests like IELTS, TOEFL, or Duolingo is strongly recommended to strengthen the student visa application and demonstrate academic readiness.
