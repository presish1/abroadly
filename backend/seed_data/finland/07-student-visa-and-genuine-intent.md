# Studying in Finland — Residence Permit and Genuine Intent

International students planning to study in Finland for more than 90 days must apply for a student residence permit, rather than a traditional visa. The application is processed by the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri). As of June 2026, students are issued a continuous residence permit (A-permit) for the entire duration of their studies [Source: Finnish Immigration Service — https://migri.fi].

## Student Residence Permit Process

1. **Pay Tuition Fees:** Pay the university tuition fee and obtain the official certificate of acceptance.
2. **Get Health Insurance:** Purchase an approved international health insurance policy.
3. **Submit Application:** Create an account on the **Enter Finland** portal, fill out the application, upload documents (acceptance letter, insurance, bank statement, fee receipt), and pay the processing fee of €350 online.
4. **Book Biometrics:** Schedule an appointment for biometrics and identity verification.
5. **Attend Appointment:** Visit the VFS Global center in Kathmandu or the Embassy of Finland in New Delhi for fingerprinting and document verification [Source: Ministry for Foreign Affairs of Finland — https://um.fi/frontpage].

## Genuine Entry and Interview Assessment

During the residence permit process, Migri assesses whether the applicant is a genuine student.
- **The Interview:** Applicants may be called for a personal interview.
- **Assessment Criteria:**
  - **Motivation:** Why did you choose Finland over other destinations? Why this specific university and major?
  - **Academic Background:** Is the chosen program a logical progression from your previous studies?
  - **Financial Situation:** Do you understand the cost of living, and are your funds genuine?
  - **Future Plans:** While having post-study career goals is acceptable, the primary focus must be on completing the degree.
- **Key Advice:** Do not use templates or memorized answers. Speak honestly about your study plans and demonstrate knowledge of the curriculum.


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
- **Question:** Are there language proficiency exemptions available?
  **Answer:** Some universities offer English proficiency waivers if the student's previous medium of instruction was entirely in English. However, taking tests like IELTS, TOEFL, or Duolingo is strongly recommended to strengthen the student visa application and demonstrate academic readiness.
