# Studying in Finland — Application Timeline and Nepali Student FAQ

Planning your studies in Finland requires careful scheduling, especially since the joint application window is open for only a short period. This guide outlines the key milestones and answers common questions from Nepali applicants. As of June 2026, tracking deadlines on the Studyinfo portal is critical [Source: Finnish National Agency for Education — https://www.studyinfo.fi].

## Month-by-Month Application Timeline

- **September - November:** Research programs and requirements. Register for and take English proficiency tests (IELTS or PTE).
- **December:** Prepare application documents (NEB certificates, transcripts, passport).
- **January:** Complete the joint application form on Studyinfo.fi. Submit before the mid-January deadline.
- **February:** Submit all required document attachments to the portal.
- **March - April:** Prepare for and participate in entrance examinations (International UAS Exam online written test and group interviews).
- **May - June:** Receive admission decisions and scholarship offers. Accept the offer and pay the first-year tuition fee (if applicable) within the deadline.
- **June - July:** Prepare bank statements, get health insurance, and apply for the residence permit on Enter Finland [Source: Finnish Immigration Service — https://migri.fi]. Attend biometrics in Kathmandu (VFS) or New Delhi.
- **August:** Receive the residence permit card, book flights, arrange housing, and arrive in Finland.

## Frequently Asked Questions

- **Can my parents sponsor my residence permit?** Only if they transfer the €9,600 directly into your personal bank account. Migri does not accept sponsor letters or bank guarantees.
- **Do I need to speak Finnish to study?** No, degree courses are taught entirely in English. However, learning basic Finnish is highly recommended for part-time work and social integration.
- **Can I bring my family?** Yes, students can apply for residence permits for their spouse and children, provided they show additional financial resources for each dependent.


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
- **Question:** Are there language proficiency exemptions available?
  **Answer:** Some universities offer English proficiency waivers if the student's previous medium of instruction was entirely in English. However, taking tests like IELTS, TOEFL, or Duolingo is strongly recommended to strengthen the student visa application and demonstrate academic readiness.
