# Studying in Ireland — Work Rights and Student Life

Living in Ireland offers international students a vibrant, welcoming environment combined with a rich cultural experience. While studying is your primary objective, understanding your rights to work part-time can help ease financial pressure and provide valuable work experience. As of June 2026, international students holding an Irish Residence Permit with Stamp 2 permission are legally allowed to work up to 20 hours per week during term time [Source: Irish Immigration Service — https://www.irishimmigration.ie/].

## Part-Time Work Rights for Stamp 2 Holders

The work rights of international students in Ireland are strictly regulated based on the academic calendar:

- **Term Time (Part-Time):** Students can work a maximum of 20 hours per week.
- **Holidays (Full-Time):** Students can work up to 40 hours per week during designated holiday periods:
  - **Summer Holidays:** June, July, August, and September.
  - **Winter Holidays:** 15 December to 15 January.
- **Stamp 2A Warning:** Students on language courses that are not listed on the Interim List of Eligible Programmes (ILEP) may receive a Stamp 2A visa, which does not allow any part-time work. Ensure your university course is Stamp 2 eligible [Source: Irish Immigration Service — https://www.irishimmigration.ie/coming-to-study-in-ireland/].

## Finding Work and the Personal Public Service (PPS) Number

To work legally and pay taxes in Ireland, you must follow several administrative steps:

- **PPS Number:** A Personal Public Service (PPS) number is a unique reference number used for tax, social welfare, and employment. You can only apply for a PPS number after arriving in Ireland, registering with immigration, and securing a job offer.
- **National Minimum Wage:** Ireland has a high national minimum wage (currently €12.70 per hour for adults, subject to annual increases).
- **Finding Jobs:** Students can find part-time work in hospitality, retail, customer service, or tutoring. Universities also host career offices that advertise on-campus roles (such as library assistants or student ambassadors).

## Cost of Living and Student Accommodation

The accommodation market in Ireland, particularly in Dublin, is highly competitive.

- **Types of Accommodation:**
  - **On-Campus Dorms:** Convenient but expensive (€600 to €1,000 per month). Must be booked months in advance.
  - **Off-Campus Student Residences:** Purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) offering modern amenities, costing €700 to €1,100 per month.
  - **Shared Housing (House Share):** The most economical option, where students rent a room in a shared house, costing €400 to €800 per month.
- **Dublin vs. Regional Cities:** Living outside Dublin (e.g., Limerick, Athlone, Sligo) can reduce your rent by 30% to 50% [Source: Education in Ireland — https://www.educationinireland.com/].

## Campus Life and Integration

Irish universities are famous for their lively student organizations and active campus communities.

- **Clubs and Societies:** Joining student-run clubs (sports, arts, debating, gaming) is the best way to integrate, meet local Irish students, and build a social network.
- **The Student Leap Card:** A transport card offering up to 50% discount on public buses, trains, and light rail (Luas) services across Ireland.
- **Student Unions:** Every campus has an active Student Union (SU) that represents student interests, organizes social events, and provides free welfare and academic advice.
