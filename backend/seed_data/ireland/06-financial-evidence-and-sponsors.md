# Studying in Ireland — Financial Evidence and Sponsors

Proving that you have sufficient financial resources is a critical requirement for securing an Irish student visa. The Irish Immigration Service must be satisfied that you can support yourself throughout your course without relying on public funds or part-time work. As of June 2026, the immigration service has updated its financial requirements, requiring visa applicants to show access to at least €10,000 per year of study for living costs, in addition to fully paid tuition fees [Source: Irish Immigration Service — https://www.irishimmigration.ie/].

## Visa Financial Requirements: The General Rules

When applying for an Irish student visa from Nepal, you must provide clear evidence of your financial capacity:

- **Living Expenses Proof:** You must show that you have immediate access to at least €10,000 for each year of your course. For a 1-year master's program, this means €10,000. For multi-year bachelor's programs, you must show €10,000 for the first year and demonstrate a clear plan/source for funding the subsequent years.
- **Tuition Fee Payment:** You must prove that you have paid at least €6,000 (or the full first-year tuition fee if it is less than €6,000) before applying for the visa. Most universities recommend paying the full first-year fee to strengthen the visa application.
- **Source of Funds:** You must show a history of funds in your bank accounts for at least 6 months prior to the application. Large lump-sum deposits made right before the visa application without a clear source will lead to rejection.

## The Student Bond Scheme (Alternative Proof)

As an alternative to traditional bank statements, Nepali students can use an approved Student Bond Scheme.

- **What is it?** A financial bond where the student deposits the required living expenses (€10,000) into an approved Irish financial institution (e.g., via platforms like EduStep or university-specific escrow systems).
- **Benefit:** The funds are held securely in Ireland and released to the student in monthly installments once they arrive, acting as undeniable proof of financial capability for the visa office.

## Sponsor Regulations and Documenting Income

If your family is sponsoring your education, you must document their financial capacity thoroughly.

- **Eligible Sponsors:** Immediate family members (parents, siblings, grandparents) are preferred sponsors. If using extended family, you must clearly explain the relationship and motivation.
- **Sponsor Documentation:**
  - **Sponsor Letters:** Signed letters confirming their commitment to cover your tuition and living costs.
  - **Bank Statements:** 6 months of detailed personal/business bank statements.
  - **Income Proof:** Tax clearance certificates, property valuation reports, business registration documents, and salary slips.
  - **Relation Proof:** Official birth certificates and relationship certificates issued by local municipalities in Nepal.

## Educational Loans and Outward Remittance from Nepal

Many Nepali students fund their studies in Ireland through educational loans.

- **Education Loan from Nepal:** Loans from recognized commercial A-class banks in Nepal are accepted. The loan approval letter, disbursement details, and mortgage/collateral valuation must be submitted with the visa application.
- **No Objection Certificate (NOC):** Under Nepal Rastra Bank regulations, you must present your NOC and university fee invoice to your commercial bank in Nepal to execute the SWIFT transfer for tuition and living expenses [Source: Ministry of Education Nepal — https://moest.gov.np/].
