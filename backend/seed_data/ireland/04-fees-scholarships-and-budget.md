# Studying in Ireland — Fees, Scholarships, and Budget

Financing your studies is a key aspect of preparing to study in Ireland. While tuition and living costs are competitive compared to the UK and USA, students must carefully plan their budgets. As of June 2026, tuition fees vary significantly between public universities, technological universities, and private colleges, and proof of sufficient funds is required for the student visa [Source: Irish Immigration Service — https://www.irishimmigration.ie/].

## Tuition Fees for International Students

Tuition fees in Ireland are set by individual institutions and depend on the course level, subject, and university type. Generally, medicine and clinical courses are the most expensive, while humanities and business are more affordable.

- **Public Universities:**
  - **Undergraduate (Bachelor's):** €10,000 to €25,000 per year.
  - **Postgraduate (Master's):** €12,000 to €30,000 per year.
- **Technological Universities:**
  - **Undergraduate:** €9,500 to €15,000 per year.
  - **Postgraduate:** €10,000 to €16,000 per year.
- **Private Colleges:**
  - **Undergraduate:** €8,000 to €12,000 per year.
  - **Postgraduate:** €9,000 to €14,000 per year [Source: Education in Ireland — https://www.educationinireland.com/].

## Scholarships for Nepali Students

A range of funding options is available to help offset tuition costs.

- **Government of Ireland International Education Scholarships (GOI-IES):**
  - **Value:** Full tuition waiver plus a €10,000 stipend for one year of study at bachelor's or master's level.
  - **Application:** Highly competitive; open to all non-EU students who have received an admission offer.
- **Institutional Scholarships:**
  - Most Irish universities offer merit-based scholarships ranging from €1,000 to €5,000 as partial tuition discounts. These are automatically assessed during admission or require a short essay.
- **Centenary Scholarship Program:**
  - Specifically targeted at students from developing countries, offered by various technological universities.

## Monthly Living Costs and Budgeting

Living expenses in Ireland vary by location. Dublin is significantly more expensive than regional cities such as Cork, Galway, Limerick, or Athlone.

- **Average Monthly Budget:**
  - **Dublin:** €1,200 to €1,800 per month (primarily driven by high accommodation costs).
  - **Outside Dublin:** €900 to €1,200 per month.
- **Breakdown of Monthly Expenses:**
  - **Rent (Shared Room/WG):** €500 to €900.
  - **Food/Groceries:** €200 to €300.
  - **Utilities (Electricity, Internet):** €80 to €120.
  - **Local Transport:** €50 to €80 (using the Student Leap Card).
  - **Leisure and Miscellaneous:** €100 to €200.

## Mandatory Student Fees and Other Upfront Costs

In addition to tuition, students must plan for several upfront and ongoing administrative fees:

- **Student Contribution Fee:** Some universities charge an annual student levy of €200 to €300 for student services and sports facilities.
- **Medical Insurance:** Mandatory health insurance costs roughly €150 to €300 per year.
- **Irish Residence Permit (IRP):** After arrival, registration with immigration costs €300 annually [Source: Irish Immigration Service — https://www.irishimmigration.ie/].
