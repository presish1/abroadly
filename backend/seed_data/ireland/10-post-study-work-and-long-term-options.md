# Studying in Ireland — Post-Study Work and Long-Term Options

Ireland's post-study pathways make it one of the most attractive destinations for ambitious international students. The Irish government actively seeks to retain talented graduates, particularly in science, technology, engineering, and business sectors. As of June 2026, the Third Level Graduate Scheme provides a clear and structured transition from study to professional employment and long-term residency [Source: Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment — https://enterprise.gov.ie/].

## The Third Level Graduate Scheme (Stamp 1G)

The Third Level Graduate Scheme allows non-EU graduates of recognized Irish institutions to remain in Ireland and work full-time without requiring a formal work permit.

- **Eligibility & Duration:**
  - **Bachelor's Graduates (NFQ Level 8):** Granted a 12-month Stamp 1G extension.
  - **Master's and PhD Graduates (NFQ Level 9/10):** Granted a 24-month Stamp 1G extension (initially issued for 12 months and renewable for another 12 months) [Source: Irish Immigration Service — https://www.irishimmigration.ie/].
- **Work Rights:** During the Stamp 1G period, you can work up to 40 hours per week in any field. The primary objective is to use this time to find a professional role that qualifies for an employment permit.

## Transitioning to Employment Permits

To remain in Ireland after your Stamp 1G expires, you must secure a job that qualifies for one of the two main employment permits issued by the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment (DETE).

1. **General Employment Permit:**
   - Valid for a wide variety of occupations.
   - Requires a minimum salary (typically €34,000 per year, subject to adjustments).
   - Requires a Labor Market Needs Test (proving that the employer could not find an Irish or EU citizen to fill the role).
2. **Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP):**
   - Highly sought-after permit for specialized professions where there is a shortage of skills in Ireland.

## Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP)

The Critical Skills Employment Permit is the fastest pathway to permanent residency in Ireland.

- **Eligible Roles:** Occupations listed on the **Critical Skills Occupations List**, including software engineers, ICT professionals, nurses, pharmaceutical scientists, and accountants [Source: Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment — https://enterprise.gov.ie/].
- **Salary Thresholds:** Minimum salary of €38,000 per year for jobs on the Critical Skills List with a relevant degree, or €64,000 per year for other roles.
- **Key Benefit:** No Labor Market Needs Test is required. More importantly, it allows family reunification, and after 2 years on a CSEP, you can apply for a Stamp 4 residency permit.

## Long-Term Residency and Citizenship

Transitioning to Stamp 4 is a major milestone for long-term integration in Ireland.

- **Stamp 4 Permit:** Grants you permission to live and work in Ireland without any work permit restriction. You are treated similarly to an Irish citizen in terms of employment. You qualify for Stamp 4 after 2 years on a Critical Skills Employment Permit or 5 years on a General Employment Permit.
- **Naturalization (Citizenship):** You can apply for Irish citizenship through naturalization after completing 5 years of reckonable residency (years spent on Stamp 1G do not count, but years spent on Stamp 4 do count towards citizenship) [Source: Irish Immigration Service — https://www.irishimmigration.ie/].
