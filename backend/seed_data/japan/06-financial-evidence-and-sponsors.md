# Studying in Japan — Financial Evidence and Sponsors

To secure a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) and a student visa for Japan, applicants must demonstrate sufficient financial capacity to cover tuition fees, accommodation, and living expenses. Japanese immigration authorities require comprehensive documentation to verify the genuineness and stability of the student's financial sponsorship. As of June 2026, the Immigration Services Agency of Japan has strict verification protocols for South Asian applicants to prevent students from entering the country under unsustainable financial burdens [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].

---

## Sponsor Eligibility and Requirements

A financial sponsor is the person responsible for funding the student's education and living costs in Japan.

* **Acceptable Sponsors:** The sponsor should ideally be a close family member (parents, siblings, or grandparents). Third-party sponsors or distant relatives are highly scrutinized and generally discouraged by immigration unless a strong, verifiable explanation is provided.
* **Co-Sponsorship:** In some cases, two close relatives (e.g., father and mother) can act as co-sponsors to meet the minimum income requirements, provided both submit complete documentation.
* **Sponsor Commitments:** The sponsor must sign a **Written Oath for Defraying Expenses**, stating their commitment to pay tuition and support the student with a monthly allowance, typically around JPY 80,000 to JPY 100,000.

---

## Bank Balance Requirements

A bank balance certificate and the associated statements are primary pieces of evidence.

* **Minimum Balance:** The sponsor must show a bank balance of at least **JPY 2,000,000 to JPY 3,000,000** (equivalent to approximately NPR 1.8 million to NPR 2.7 million). This amount represents the estimated total tuition and living costs for one full academic year.
* **Approved Banks:** The funds must be held in a recognized commercial bank (Class 'A' banks in Nepal approved by Nepal Rastra Bank). Co-operative societies and finance companies are generally not accepted by Japanese immigration.
* **Statement Duration:** A minimum of **3 years of bank statements** (some schools accept 1–2 years, but 3 years is the gold standard for immigration) must be provided. This statement must reflect active, regular transactions. Sudden, unexplained large deposits right before the application will lead to rejection.

---

## Income Sources and Verification

The sponsor must prove they have a stable, ongoing source of income that matches the bank transactions.

* **Minimum Annual Income:** The sponsor's total annual income should ideally be at least **JPY 1,000,000 to JPY 1,500,000** (approx. NPR 900,000 to NPR 1.3 million).
* **Employment Income:** If the sponsor is an employee, they must provide:
  * **Salary Certificate:** Printed on the employer’s official letterhead, showing designation, date of joining, and monthly/annual salary breakdown.
  * **Tax Clearance Certificate:** Documenting taxes paid on salary for the last 3 fiscal years.
* **Business Income:** If the sponsor owns a business, they must provide:
  * **Business Registration:** Official registration certificate from the Office of the Company Registrar or Department of Cottage and Small Industries.
  * **Audit Reports & Tax Clearance:** Audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates for the last 3 fiscal years.
* **Agriculture & Land Income:** For sponsors whose primary income is agriculture, they must provide:
  * **Land Ownership Certificates (Lalpurja):** Documenting land holdings.
  * **Municipality Income Verification:** Official letters from the local ward office certifying agricultural yield and annual income, along with local tax payment receipts.
