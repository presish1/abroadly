# Studying in Japan — Pathways and Entry Requirements

For Nepali students wishing to study in Japan, there are clear pathways to entry, depending on their current academic standing and Japanese language proficiency. Because Japan's higher education system operates primarily in Japanese, navigating these pathways requires careful planning. As of June 2026, admission standards have become more rigorous to ensure that arriving students can keep up with academic coursework and integrate successfully into Japanese society [Source: Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology — https://www.mext.go.jp/en/].

---

## Pathway 1: The Japanese Language School Pathway

This is the most common route chosen by Nepali students. Since direct entry into Japanese-taught undergraduate or vocational programs requires high-level language skills, students first enroll in an intensive Japanese language school in Japan.

* **Purpose:** To transition from basic Japanese (N5/N4 level) to advanced Japanese (N2/N1 level), which is necessary for university study and employment.
* **Duration:** 1 to 2 years, depending on the intake month (April intake is typically 2 years; October intake is 1.5 years; July intake is 1 year and 9 months; January intake is 1 year and 3 months).
* **Progression:** Upon graduating from the language school, students sit for the EJU (Examination for Japanese University Admission for International Students) or individual university entrance exams to progress to a bachelor's degree, master's degree, or vocational training at a Senmon Gakko [Source: Japan Student Services Organization — https://www.jasso.go.jp/en/].

---

## Pathway 2: Direct Admission to English-Medium Programs

An increasing number of Japanese universities offer degree programs taught entirely in English, particularly at the global-focused national and top private universities.

* **Undergraduate Level:** Programs such as the MEXT-funded global initiatives allow students to apply directly from Nepal.
* **Graduate Level (Master's and PhD):** Many STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Math) and business programs are offered in English.
* **Advantage:** No prior Japanese language knowledge is required for admission, although basic Japanese is highly recommended for daily survival.

---

## Academic Requirements

To apply for any educational program in Japan, students must meet minimum academic benchmarks:

* **Undergraduate (Bachelor's and Associate Degrees):** Students must have completed 12 years of formal school education (Class 12 / NEB or equivalent). A minimum GPA of 2.4 out of 4.0 (or approximately 50-60%) in the School Leaving Certificate (SLC) / Class 12 exams is generally required, though top universities demand higher scores [Source: Study in Japan Portal — https://www.studyinjapan.go.jp/].
* **Graduate Programs (Master's and PhD):** For a master's program, a completed 16-year education (a 4-year bachelor's degree) is required. For a doctoral program, an 18-year education (a master's degree) is required. Universities look for strong academic records, a research proposal, and a relevant bachelor's major.
* **Vocational Schools (Senmon Gakko):** Requires completion of 12 years of formal education and a basic academic record.

## Language Proficiency Requirements

Language capability is the single most critical factor for visa and admission approval. The requirements vary based on the chosen pathway:

### 1. Requirements for Japanese Language Schools
To obtain a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) for a language school, Nepali students must demonstrate basic Japanese capability. This can be proven through:
* **Test Scores:** A pass certificate in standard tests such as NAT-TEST (5Q or higher), JLPT (N5 or higher), J-CERT, or TOP-J.
* **Study Hours:** A minimum of 150 hours of Japanese language study at a recognized language institute in Nepal, backed by an official certificate of completion [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].

### 2. Requirements for Japanese-Medium Higher Education
To enter a university or vocational school directly or after language school:
* **JLPT Score:** Minimum N2 level for vocational programs and humanities/social sciences. N1 is highly preferred for competitive universities and corporate employment.
* **EJU (Examination for Japanese University Admission):** Standardized test testing Japanese language, science, mathematics, and "Japan and the World" subject areas [Source: Japan Student Services Organization — https://www.jasso.go.jp/en/].

### 3. Requirements for English-Medium Programs
* **English Tests:** IELTS Academic (minimum overall band of 6.0 with no band below 5.5, though 6.5 is preferred for top institutions) or TOEFL iBT (minimum score of 80).
* **Medium of Instruction (MOI) Certificate:** Some universities accept an MOI certificate from a student's bachelor's institution in Nepal, though official test scores are much stronger.
