# Studying in Japan — Student Visa and Genuine Intent

Securing a student visa for Japan is a two-step process: obtaining a Certificate of Eligibility (COE) from the Immigration Services Agency in Japan and subsequently applying for the visa stamp at the Embassy of Japan in Nepal. Immigration and consular officers assess applications not only on paper compliance but also on the applicant's "genuine student intent." As of June 2026, the scrutiny of visa applications remains strict, requiring students to demonstrate clear academic purpose and basic language proficiency [Source: Embassy of Japan in Nepal — https://www.np.embassy-japan.go.jp/].

---

## Step 1: The Certificate of Eligibility (COE) Process

The COE is a document issued by the Ministry of Justice in Japan verifying that the applicant meets the entry requirements for foreign nationals.

1. **Submission:** The hosting Japanese language school or university submits the student's complete document package (academic records, personal details, and sponsor's financial proofs) to the local Immigration Services Agency office in Japan.
2. **Assessment:** Immigration reviews the documents over a 2 to 3-month period. They look closely for inconsistent data, questionable financial sponsor backgrounds, or unrealistic educational paths.
3. **Approval and Tuition Payment:** Once the COE is approved, a digital or physical copy is sent to the school. The school then issues an invoice for the first year's tuition. After the student transfers the tuition fee via a bank in Nepal using their NOC, the school mails the original physical COE and the Letter of Admission to Nepal [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].

---

## Step 2: The Visa Application at the Embassy of Japan in Nepal

Once the student receives the physical COE, they must apply for the student visa at the Embassy of Japan in Kathmandu.

* **Visa Application Submission:** The student submits the original COE, Letter of Admission, passport, visa application form, and passport-size photographs.
* **Embassy Interview:** The Embassy frequently conducts personal interviews for Nepali applicants. The interview can be conducted in Japanese or English, depending on the program's medium of instruction.
* **Decision:** If the embassy is satisfied with the documents and the interview, the visa is stamped on the passport. The processing time is typically 5 to 10 working days.

---

## Demonstrating Genuine Student Intent

To pass the embassy interview and avoid visa rejection, students must prove they are genuine students whose primary goal is education, not work:

### 1. Clear Academic Goals and Plan
Students should be able to articulate:
* The exact name, location, and history of the school they will attend in Japan.
* The structure of their course, the number of hours they will study, and what they will do after completing their initial course (e.g., transitioning from a language school to a specific university major).
* Why they chose Japan over other popular destinations.

### 2. Conversational Language Skills
For students going to Japanese language schools, the embassy officer will likely test basic conversational Japanese. Students must be able to introduce themselves (Jikoshoukai), answer questions about their family, hobby, and sponsors, and read simple Japanese characters (Hiragana, Katakana, and basic Kanji).

### 3. Understanding the Rules and Regulations
Students must demonstrate an understanding of Japan's immigration rules:
* Acknowledging that they are legally allowed to work a maximum of 28 hours per week and that this work must not interfere with their school attendance.
* Stating their commitment to maintaining an attendance rate of 90% or higher.
* Showing a clear intention to either return to Nepal after completing their studies or transition to a regular work visa through legal channels [Source: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan — https://www.mofa.go.jp/].
