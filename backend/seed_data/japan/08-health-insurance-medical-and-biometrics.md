# Studying in Japan — Health Insurance, Medical, and Biometrics

Navigating the medical and administrative systems of a new country is a vital part of preparing for study abroad. Japan has highly organized systems for health insurance, medical checks, and biometric identification that apply to all international students. As of June 2026, compliance with these procedures is strictly monitored by both the local city offices and immigration authorities to ensure public health and security [Source: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare of Japan — https://www.mhlw.go.jp/english/].

---

## National Health Insurance (NHI)

In Japan, all residents, including international students on a student visa staying longer than 3 months, are legally required to enroll in the **National Health Insurance (NHI)** system (Kokumin Kenko Hoken).

* **Registration:** Students must register for the NHI at the local ward office (Kuyakusho) or city hall (Shiyakusho) of their neighborhood when registering their residential address.
* **Benefits:** Once enrolled, the NHI covers **70% of medical expenses** for illnesses, injuries, and dental work. The student is only responsible for paying the remaining 30% of the cost at clinics or hospitals.
* **Premiums:** The monthly insurance premium is based on the student's income in Japan from the previous year. For newly arrived Nepali students with no prior Japanese income, the premium is heavily discounted, typically costing around **JPY 1,500 to JPY 2,500 per month**. Failing to pay premiums can lead to visa renewal rejections and penalties.
* **Private Insurance:** Some schools recommend or require students to purchase additional private travel or personal liability insurance for the first few months or to cover the 30% copay, but this does not replace the mandatory NHI [Source: Japan Student Services Organization — https://www.jasso.go.jp/en/].

## Medical Examinations and Health Checks

Japan maintains strict public health protocols, particularly concerning infectious diseases:

* **Pre-Departure Medical Requirements:** While Japan does not require a general medical examination for all visa applicants, individual universities and language schools often require a health certificate. This must be completed by a certified medical center in Nepal.
* **Tuberculosis (TB) Screening:** Because Nepal has a relatively high incidence of tuberculosis, Japanese immigration and schools require specific screening, including a chest X-ray. If a student is suspected of having active TB, their admission and entry will be deferred until they are cleared.
* **On-Campus Health Checks:** Almost all Japanese universities and language schools conduct mandatory annual health check-ups for all students. These are usually free or heavily subsidized and include height, weight, eyesight, blood pressure, urine analysis, and chest X-rays.

## Biometrics and the Residence Card (Zairyu Card)

The Residence Card (Zairyu Card) is the most important document for an international student in Japan, serving as their official identification.

* **Airport Biometrics:** Upon arrival at major airports (Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, etc.), students go through immigration, where their biometrics (fingerprints and a digital facial photograph) are recorded.
* **Card Issuance:** At the airport, the immigration officer prints and issues the physical **Zairyu Card** immediately.
* **Address Registration (Anmeldung equivalent):** Within 14 days of finding permanent accommodation, the student must take their Zairyu Card to the local ward office or city hall to register their address. The office will print the address on the back of the card. This step is mandatory before a student can open a bank account or sign a mobile phone contract [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].
