# Studying in Japan — Application Timeline and Nepali Student FAQ

Planning a study journey to Japan requires careful adherence to structured timelines. Because Japanese immigration works on fixed cycles, missing a deadline can delay an applicant's plans by six months to a year. Additionally, Nepali students frequently share common queries regarding eligibility, age restrictions, dependent visas, and career changes. As of June 2026, the application timeline and policy clarifications are updated to reflect the latest embassy guidelines [Source: Embassy of Japan in Nepal — https://www.np.embassy-japan.go.jp/].

---

## Recommended Timeline for April Intake (Primary Intake)

The April intake is the most popular and has the largest number of seats. Below is the ideal 12-month preparation timeline:

* **April – June (Previous Year):** Research Japanese language schools or universities. Begin basic Japanese language classes in Nepal to prepare for the N5/NAT-TEST. Collect academic transcripts and passport.
* **July – August (Previous Year):** Select the target school and submit the initial application. Sit for the NAT-TEST or JLPT exam. Pay the school's screening fee (typically JPY 20,000 to JPY 30,000).
* **September – October (Previous Year):** Prepare the complete document package, including the sponsor's financial certificates, tax clearance documents, relationship verification, and translation of Nepali documents.
* **November (Previous Year):** The school in Japan submits the student's document package and COE application to the regional Japanese Immigration Services Agency office.
* **Late February (Intake Year):** Immigration announces the COE results. Upon approval, the student receives a scanned copy of the COE and the school's tuition invoice.
* **Early March (Intake Year):** Apply for the No Objection Certificate (NOC) from MoEST Nepal. Transfer the tuition fee via bank wire transfer (Telegraphic Transfer - TT). The school mails the original physical COE.
* **Mid-March (Intake Year):** Submit the passport, visa application form, and physical COE to the Embassy of Japan in Kathmandu for visa processing. Book flight tickets.
* **Late March – Early April (Intake Year):** Receive the visa-stamped passport. Attend pre-departure orientation, pack, and fly to Japan. Register residence and open a bank account [Source: Japan Student Services Organization — https://www.jasso.go.jp/en/].

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) for Nepali Students

### Q1: Can I apply if I have a study gap after Class 12 or Bachelor's?
* **Answer:** Yes. A study gap of 2 to 5 years is generally acceptable, provided the student can explain what they did during the gap. This must be backed by official documents such as work experience certificates, training certificates, or professional registrations. Gaps without verifiable activities are viewed negatively by immigration.

### Q2: Is there an official age limit to apply for a student visa?
* **Answer:** Officially, there is no age limit. However, applicants over 30 years old applying for language schools face much higher scrutiny. They must provide a very strong explanation of how learning Japanese fits into their career progression, along with extensive work history.

### Q3: Can I bring my spouse (dependent) with me to Japan?
* **Answer:** Students enrolled in Japanese language schools cannot bring dependents (spouse or children). However, students enrolled in undergraduate degrees, master's degrees, or PhD programs at universities can sponsor a **Dependent Visa**, provided they can prove they have sufficient financial resources to support them without relying on part-time work.

### Q4: What happens if my COE is rejected?
* **Answer:** If a COE is rejected, the student receives a rejection code indicating the reason (e.g., lack of financial credibility, inconsistent documents, or insufficient language proof). Students can rectify the issues and re-apply for the next intake, either through the same school or a different one.

### Q5: Can I change my course or university after arriving in Japan?
* **Answer:** Changing schools while on a language school visa is very difficult and generally not allowed unless the school closes. However, upon graduating from the language school, students are free to apply to any university or vocational school of their choice, subject to meeting the entry requirements [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].
