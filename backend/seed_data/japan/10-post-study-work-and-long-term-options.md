# Studying in Japan — Post-Study Work and Long-Term Options

For many Nepali students, the ultimate goal of studying in Japan is to transition into the Japanese workforce and establish a long-term career. Japan's severe demographic decline has led the government to ease the transition from student status to professional work visas. However, students must navigate a highly structured employment system and satisfy immigration requirements to change their visa status. As of June 2026, the job-hunting timelines and visa options remain highly standardized [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].

---

## The Japanese Job Hunting Process (Shukatsu)

Job hunting in Japan, known as *Shukatsu*, is a highly formalized and time-sensitive process that differs significantly from Western countries.

* **Timeline:** University students typically begin job hunting in their third year of bachelor's studies (around March) to secure job offers (*Naitei*) before graduating in March of their fourth year. For vocational school (Senmon Gakko) and language school students, the process starts in their final year.
* **Key Components:**
  * **Entry Sheets (ES):** Detailed applications where students write essays about their motivations and self-promotions.
  * **SPI Tests:** Standardized aptitude exams testing mathematics, Japanese language comprehension, and personality traits.
  * **Interviews:** Usually consists of three to four rounds, including group discussions, HR interviews, and executive interviews.
* **Language Prerequisite:** Standard corporate jobs require Business Japanese. Students must typically hold a JLPT N1 certificate (N2 is acceptable for some IT positions where technical skills are paramount).

---

## Primary Work Visa: Gijinkoku Visa

The most common work visa for university and vocational graduates is the **Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services** visa, commonly abbreviated as *Gijinkoku*.

* **Eligibility:** Applicants must have graduated from a Japanese university (Bachelor's or Master's) or a Professional Training College with a "Senmon-shi" (Diploma) or "Kodo Senmon-shi" (Advanced Diploma).
* **Academic Alignment:** The job description must align directly with the graduate's field of study. For example, a business graduate can work in sales or marketing, while an IT graduate can work as a software engineer. A mismatch between the major and the job duties will result in visa denial.
* **Contract Requirement:** The applicant must have a signed contract with a registered company in Japan, and the salary must be equal to or higher than that of a Japanese national in the same position [Source: Ministry of Justice of Japan — https://www.moj.go.jp/EN/].

---

## Specified Skilled Worker (SSW - Tokutei Ginou) Visa

Introduced to combat labor shortages, the SSW visa is an alternative pathway, especially for vocational and language school students:

* **Target Fields:** Caregiving, food service, agriculture, construction, hospitality, aviation, and manufacturing.
* **Prerequisites:** Students must pass two exams:
  1. The industry-specific skills assessment test.
  2. The Japanese Foundation Test (JFT-Basic) or JLPT N4 or higher.
* **Education:** A university degree is not required. Language school graduates who do not wish to go to university can transition directly to SSW if they pass the exams [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].

---

## Job-Seeker Visa (Designated Activities)

If a student graduates from a Japanese university or Senmon Gakko without securing a job, they can apply for a **Designated Activities** visa for job hunting:

* **Duration:** Valid for 6 months, renewable once for a maximum of 1 year.
* **Privileges:** Allows the graduate to remain in Japan to continue job hunting. With school recommendation, they can also obtain a part-time work permit of 28 hours per week during this period [Source: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan — https://www.mofa.go.jp/].

---

## Permanent Residency and the HSP Point System

* **Standard Route:** Generally, 10 years of continuous residence in Japan is required, with at least 5 of those years on a work visa.
* **Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) Point System:** A fast-track system where applicants gain points for academic degrees, professional experience, age, annual salary, and Japanese language scores. Accumulating 70 points reduces the residency requirement to 3 years; accumulating 80 points reduces it to just 1 year [Source: Immigration Services Agency of Japan — https://www.isa.go.jp/en/].
