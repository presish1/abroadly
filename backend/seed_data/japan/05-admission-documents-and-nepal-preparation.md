# Studying in Japan — Admission Documents and Nepal Preparation

The application process for Japanese institutions requires meticulous documentation. Immigration authorities and educational institutions in Japan inspect documents thoroughly, and any discrepancy, error, or suspected forgery can lead to immediate rejection. For Nepali students, preparation starts months in advance with gathering, translating, and legalizing personal and academic records. As of June 2026, the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology in Nepal and the Embassy of Japan have streamlined processes, but strict document verification remains in place [Source: Embassy of Japan in Nepal — https://www.np.embassy-japan.go.jp/].

---

## Academic Document Requirements

Students must present complete records of their academic history. Standard academic requirements include:

* **Secondary and Higher Secondary Documents:** Grades 10 (SEE/SLC) and 12 (NEB) transcripts, character certificates, and provisional/migration certificates.
* **Higher Education Documents (if applicable):** Transcripts, character certificates, and provisional/degree certificates for bachelor's or master's degrees.
* **Japanese Language Study Certificate:** For Japanese language schools, an official certificate showing a minimum of 150 hours of Japanese language training at a registered language institute in Nepal is required.
* **Language Test Score Reports:** The original score sheet of JLPT (N5 or higher) or NAT-TEST (5Q or higher) is critical for language school pathways. For English-medium programs, an official IELTS or TOEFL score report must be sent directly to the university [Source: Japan Student Services Organization — https://www.jasso.go.jp/en/].

---

## Personal and Relationship Documents

Because Japanese immigration places a strong emphasis on sponsorship, proving relationships and identity is crucial:

* **Valid Passport:** Must have at least 1-2 years of validity remaining.
* **Photographs:** Standard passport-sized photos with a white background, taken within the last 3 months, with strict dimension requirements.
* **Birth Certificate:** Issued by the local municipality, showing date and place of birth.
* **Relationship Certificate:** A vital document issued by the local municipality that officially verifies the familial relationship between the applicant and their financial sponsor (usually parents).
* **Citizenship Certificate:** The applicant’s and the sponsor's Nepali citizenship certificates, which must match the passport details.
* **Address Verification:** If the current permanent address differs from that on older documents, an address verification certificate from the local ward office is required.

---

## Translation and Notarization

All documents originally written in Nepali (such as citizenship certificates, relationship certificates, and local ward documents) must undergo formal translation:

* **Language of Translation:** Translated into Japanese (strongly preferred for language schools and immigration) or English.
* **Authorized Translators:** Translations must be done by certified translation agencies or language institutes recognized by the Ministry of Education or the Association of Nepalese Language Schools.
* **Notarization:** Copies of the original documents and their translations must be notarized by a registered notary public in Nepal to certify their authenticity.

---

## No Objection Certificate (NOC) from Nepal Government

The No Objection Certificate (NOC) is a mandatory document issued by the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MoEST) of Nepal.

* **Purpose:** The NOC is required by Nepali banks to authorize foreign currency exchange (payment of tuition fees and living expenses to Japan) and by immigration authorities at Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) prior to departure.
* **Application Process:** Students must apply online via the MoEST NOC portal, uploading their offer letter (Letter of Admission), academic documents, and citizenship.
* **Fee:** A standard application fee of NPR 2,000 must be paid online or through designated banks.
* **Verification:** Once approved online, students must visit the NOC department in Kathmandu to collect the physical certificate [Source: Ministry of Education, Science and Technology of Nepal — https://moest.gov.np/].
