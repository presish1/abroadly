# Studying in Germany — Application Timeline and Nepali Student FAQ

Planning your studies in Germany requires meticulous timing. The application chain—from language learning and document legalization to uni-assist evaluation and visa processing—takes approximately 9 to 12 months. As of June 2026, Nepali students are advised to start their preparation at least one year before their intended intake to secure university slots and embassy appointments in Kathmandu [Source: DAAD — https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/plan-your-studies/].

## The Step-by-Step Germany Application Timeline

Most German universities operate on two semesters: the **Winter Semester** (starting October) and the **Summer Semester** (starting April). Below is a typical timeline for the Winter Semester intake.

| Timeframe | Action Items | Key Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Oct – Jan** | Start German language classes in Kathmandu (GZK or similar). | Reach at least A2/B1 level. |
| **Jan – Feb** | Research programs on the DAAD database; identify entry requirements. | Prepare syllabus descriptions. |
| **Mar – Apr** | Gather academic certificates; obtain MoFA authentication. | Prepare CV and SOP. |
| **May – Jun** | Submit applications via uni-assist or direct university portals. | Pay uni-assist fees (€75 first choice). |
| **Jun – Jul** | Receive admission letter (Zulassung); open Blocked Account online. | Deposit €11,904 into Sperrkonto. |
| **Jul – Aug** | Book visa appointment via VFS/Embassy; secure travel insurance. | Prepare for visa interview. |
| **Sep** | Attend visa interview; receive visa; book one-way flight. | Plan housing in Germany. |
| **Oct** | Arrive in Germany; register address (Anmeldung); enroll at university. | Activate health insurance. |

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) for Nepali Students

**Q: Can I apply to a German university with only IELTS/TOEFL?**  
A: Yes. If you apply for an English-taught master's or bachelor's program, you only need English proficiency. However, basic German (A1/A2) is highly recommended for visa interviews and daily survival [Source: German Embassy Kathmandu — https://kathmandu.diplo.de/np-en/service].

**Q: Is there any age limit to study in Germany?**  
A: Legally, there is no age limit for university admission or student visas. However, you must explain any significant study gaps in your motivation letter and demonstrate how the degree fits your career trajectory.

**Q: Can I change my subject or university after arriving in Germany?**  
A: Changing your course of study is possible but highly regulated. It is generally allowed during the first three semesters, but you must notify the local Foreigners' Authority (Ausländerbehörde) to update your residence permit.

**Q: What is the German grade equivalence (German Scale)?**  
A: Germany uses a 1-to-5 grading scale, where 1.0 is excellent and 4.0 is the minimum passing grade. Your Nepali percentage or GPA is converted using the "Modified Bavarian Formula." Most universities require at least 2.5 or better for master's programs.

**Q: Can I cover my living expenses entirely through part-time jobs?**  
A: While part-time work helps offset daily expenses, you should not rely on it to fund your blocked account or tuition. The 140-day work limit and academic pressure make full self-funding difficult.
