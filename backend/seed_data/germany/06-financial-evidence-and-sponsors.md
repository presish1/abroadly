# Studying in Germany — Financial Evidence and Sponsors

Demonstrating sufficient financial resources is a mandatory prerequisite for obtaining a German student visa. Nepali students must prove they can cover their living expenses for at least the first year of their studies. As of June 2026, the German Federal Foreign Office mandates that student visa applicants show proof of €11,904 for one academic year [Source: Federal Foreign Office — https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service].

## The German Blocked Account (Sperrkonto)

The most common and widely accepted method to prove financial capability is the **Blocked Account (Sperrkonto)**. This is a special type of bank account opened in Germany, where the required funds are locked, and a fixed monthly amount is released to the student.

- **Required Amount:** The total required amount is €11,904.
- **Monthly Payout:** Once you arrive in Germany, you will be allowed to withdraw a maximum of €992 per month to pay for rent, food, health insurance, and other daily living expenses [Source: Federal Foreign Office — https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service].
- **Buffer Funds:** It is highly recommended to transfer an additional €150 to €250 to cover bank transfer fees and account maintenance fees charged by the blocked account provider, ensuring that the net balance does not fall below €11,904.

## Blocked Account Providers and Setup Process

Nepali students can open a blocked account online through several government-approved providers. The most popular providers include:

1. **Expatrio:** A digital platform offering blocked accounts and health insurance packages.
2. **Fintiba:** An online portal popular among international students, operating in partnership with Sutor Bank.
3. **Coracle:** Another online provider offering fast processing and digital approval certificates.
4. **Deutsche Bank:** A traditional bank that requires physical paper application submission via the embassy, which is slower and less common now.

**Steps to Open a Sperrkonto from Nepal:**
- Register on the provider's website or app and submit a scanned copy of your passport.
- Receive the account details and transfer the €11,904 from Nepal using a Nostro bank transfer.
- Once the funds are received, the provider issues a **Blocked Amount Confirmation** (Sperrbestätigung). This document is required for your visa interview.

## Alternative Financial Proof: Declaration of Commitment

If you cannot deposit funds in a blocked account, you may submit a **Declaration of Commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung)**.

- **Sponsor in Germany:** A sponsor (relative or friend) residing legally in Germany can sign this declaration at their local immigration office (Ausländerbehörde).
- **Sponsor's Requirements:** The sponsor must prove their financial stability through recent payslips, tax returns, and employment contracts showing they have enough disposable income to support you [Source: German Embassy Kathmandu — https://kathmandu.diplo.de/np-en/service].
- **Validity:** The document must specifically state that the sponsor covers all costs of your stay under sections 66-68 of the German Residence Act.

## Other Costs and Currency Exchange Regulations in Nepal

Nepali students must also prepare for upfront expenses that cannot be paid from the blocked account:

- **No Objection Letter (NOC):** To transfer the blocked amount and pay university fees, you must obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology in Nepal [Source: Ministry of Education Nepal — https://moest.gov.np/].
- **Foreign Exchange Regulations:** Under Nepal Rastra Bank rules, commercial banks in Nepal require the NOC and an admission offer letter to process the outward remittance.
- **Visa Fees and Travel Expenses:** Prepare for the embassy visa fee (€75), VFS service charges, health insurance, and one-way airfare from Kathmandu to Frankfurt or Munich.
