# Studying in Germany — Pathways and Entry Requirements for Nepali Students

Nepali students can enter the German higher education system at multiple levels, but credential recognition and language proficiency are critical gatekeepers. Germany uses the Anabin database to evaluate foreign qualifications [Source: Anabin — https://anabin.kmk.org/].

## Post-Class 12 Pathway (Bachelor's)

Nepali students who have completed Class 12 (NEB or equivalent) with strong results may qualify for direct university admission at some institutions. However, many German universities require Nepali students to complete a one-year **Studienkolleg** (preparatory course) before starting a bachelor's degree [Source: Studienkolleg — https://www.studienkollegs.de/].

**Key steps:**
1. Check your qualification on the Anabin database or use the DAAD admission database to verify whether your Class 12 is recognised for direct entry or requires Studienkolleg.
2. If Studienkolleg is required, apply to a Studienkolleg, complete the 1-year course, and pass the Feststellungsprüfung (assessment exam).
3. If direct entry is possible, apply through uni-assist or directly to the university.

**Studienkolleg course types:** T-Kurs (technical/engineering), W-Kurs (business/economics), M-Kurs (medicine/biology), G-Kurs (humanities), S-Kurs (languages). Choose based on your intended bachelor's field [Source: Studienkolleg — https://www.studienkollegs.de/].

## Bachelor's Entry Requirements

- **Academic:** Class 12 with minimum 60-70% (varies by university and whether Studienkolleg is needed). Some universities accept students with NEB results plus 1 year of bachelor's study in Nepal.
- **Language:** Most bachelor's programs require **German proficiency** — TestDaF Level 4 in all four sections (TDN 4×4) or DSH-2. A few English-taught bachelor's programs exist and require IELTS 6.0-6.5 or TOEFL 80-90 [Source: DAAD — https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/plan-your-studies/requirements/].
- **Application:** Through uni-assist (for most universities) or directly to the university. Application deadlines are typically **15 July for winter semester** (October start) and **15 January for summer semester** (April start) [Source: uni-assist — https://www.uni-assist.de/en/].

## Master's Entry Requirements

Master's programs are where Nepali students have the most options, especially for English-taught courses.

- **Academic:** Completed bachelor's degree (minimum 3 years) in a relevant field, usually with a GPA equivalent of 2.5 or better on the German scale (roughly 65%+ in Nepal's grading system). Some competitive programs require higher.
- **Language:** English-taught master's programs typically require IELTS 6.5 (no band below 6.0) or TOEFL iBT 90+. German-taught programs require TestDaF 4×4 or DSH-2. Some programs require both [Source: DAAD — https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/plan-your-studies/requirements/].
- **Other:** GRE/GMAT required for some business and engineering programs. Research proposal needed for research-track master's.

## Language Requirements Summary

| Test | Score Required | Used For |
|---|---|---|
| TestDaF | TDN 4 in all 4 sections | German-taught programs |
| DSH | DSH-2 or DSH-3 | German-taught programs |
| Goethe-Zertifikat | C1 or C2 | Some universities accept |
| IELTS Academic | 6.0–7.0 | English-taught programs |
| TOEFL iBT | 80–100 | English-taught programs |

As of June 2026, learning German to at least B1 level is strongly recommended even for English-taught programs — it helps with housing search, part-time work, and daily life [Source: DAAD — https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/plan-your-studies/learning-german/].

## PhD Pathway

PhD positions in Germany are often structured as employed research positions (Wissenschaftlicher Mitarbeiter) with a salary, rather than tuition-based programs. Search on the DAAD Research in Germany portal or individual university websites. English is widely used at the PhD level [Source: Research in Germany — https://www.research-in-germany.org/en/].
