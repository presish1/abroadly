# Studying in Germany — Health Insurance, Medical, and Biometrics

Every resident in Germany is legally required to have health insurance, and this rule is strictly enforced for international students. You cannot enroll at a German university or obtain a residence permit without proof of adequate health insurance coverage. As of June 2026, health insurance regulations require international students under the age of 30 to be insured under the public health system, unless they opt out [Source: DAAD — https://www.daad.de/en/study-and-research-in-germany/plan-your-studies/health-insurance/].

## The German Health Insurance System

Health insurance in Germany is divided into two sectors: **Statutory Public Health Insurance (Gesetzliche Krankenversicherung - GKV)** and **Private Health Insurance (Private Krankenversicherung - PKV)**.

- **Mandatory Enrollment:** When enrolling at a university, the institution will ask for an electronic notification of health insurance status (Meldung zur Krankenversicherung). This is sent electronically by the insurance provider directly to the university.
- **Travel Insurance vs. Long-term Insurance:** For the visa application in Nepal, you only need temporary travel health insurance (often called incoming insurance) covering your first 90 days. Once you arrive in Germany, you must activate your long-term German health insurance.

## Public vs. Private Health Insurance

Understanding the differences between public and private insurance is crucial for Nepali students.

- **Public Health Insurance:**
  - **Providers:** AOK, TK (Techniker Krankenkasse), Barmer, and DAK are the main providers.
  - **Eligibility:** Students under 30 enrolled in regular degree programs (bachelor's or master's) at state-recognized universities.
  - **Cost:** Approximately €120 to €130 per month.
  - **Benefits:** Excellent coverage, including general check-ups, hospital stays, prescription medications, and dental care with minimal co-payments.
- **Private Health Insurance:**
  - **Providers:** Mawista, Care Concept, and Educare.
  - **Eligibility:** Students over the age of 30, PhD candidates, language school students, or Studienkolleg students.
  - **Cost:** Often cheaper upfront (€35 to €70 per month) but may involve higher co-payments and out-of-pocket expenses [Source: German Embassy Kathmandu — https://kathmandu.diplo.de/np-en/service].
  - **Important Note:** Once you choose private insurance, you cannot easily switch back to public insurance during your studies.

## Medical Checks and Health Requirements

Unlike some countries, Germany does not require a comprehensive pre-departure medical check-up or tuberculosis (TB) screening at designated clinics in Nepal for the student visa.

- **Vaccinations:** There are no mandatory vaccine requirements for the visa, but it is highly recommended to have standard vaccinations (Measles, Mumps, Rubella, Tetanus, and Hepatitis B) up to date. The measles vaccine is mandatory if you live in student dormitories or work in healthcare/education [Source: Federal Ministry of Health — https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/en/].
- **Prescription Medication:** If you bring prescription drugs from Nepal, ensure you carry a doctor's letter in English specifying the generic name of the drug and the prescribed dosage.

## Biometrics and Visa Document Verification

During the visa application process, you must submit biometrics.

- **Biometrics Collection:** Biometric data, including a digital facial photograph and ten-finger scans, is collected during your appointment at the German Embassy or VFS Global in Kathmandu.
- **Document Verification (Urkundenprüfung):** In some cases, the German Embassy may initiate a verification process for your academic and civil documents. This process is conducted by embassy-appointed lawyers in Nepal and requires an additional fee paid by the student [Source: German Embassy Kathmandu — https://kathmandu.diplo.de/np-en/service].
