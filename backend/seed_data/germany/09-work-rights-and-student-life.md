# Studying in Germany — Work Rights and Student Life

Living and working in Germany offers an exciting opportunity for Nepali students to experience European culture while supporting their education. However, strict legal limits apply to student employment, and balancing work with academic demands requires careful planning. As of June 2026, international students are permitted to work up to 140 full days or 280 half days per calendar year [Source: Federal Foreign Office — https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service].

## Part-Time Work Regulations for International Students

Germany regulates the number of hours international students can work to ensure that their primary focus remains on their studies.

- **The 140-Day Rule:** You can work 140 full days (up to 8 hours per day) or 280 half days (up to 4 hours per day) per year. This limit was increased from 120 full days / 240 half days recently [Source: Federal Foreign Office — https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service].
- **Term-Time vs. Vacation:** During the semester, students are generally restricted to working 20 hours per week. During semester breaks, they can work full-time (up to 40 hours per week), provided they do not exceed the annual 140-day limit.
- **Self-Employment:** Freelancing or self-employment is strictly prohibited for international students on a student visa, unless explicit permission is granted by the local Foreigners' Authority (Ausländerbehörde).

## Types of Part-Time Jobs and Wages

Nepali students can find work in various sectors, but the type of job often depends on their language skills.

- **Student Assistant (HiWi):** Working at the university as a Research Assistant (Wissenschaftliche Hilfskraft) or Student Assistant (Studentische Hilfskraft) in labs, libraries, or administrative departments. These jobs are highly relevant academically and are exempt from the 140-day limit.
- **Minijobs:** Part-time positions in retail, hospitality, delivery services, or warehouses. As of 2024/2025, Minijobs allow earning up to €538 per month tax-free.
- **Minimum Wage:** The national minimum wage in Germany is legally mandated (currently €12.41 per hour, subject to regular adjustments). Even with basic German, students can earn enough to cover their monthly groceries and utilities.
- **Taxation:** If you earn more than the tax-free allowance, you must pay income tax and social security contributions, though much of this can be refunded by filing a tax return.

## Cost of Living and Student Accommodation

Managing living costs is a major part of student life. While tuition is free at public universities, living expenses vary by city.

- **Accommodation Options:**
  - **Student Dormitories (Studentenwerk):** The cheapest option, costing €250 to €400 per month. However, waiting lists are extremely long (often 1-2 semesters).
  - **Shared Apartments (Wohngemeinschaft - WG):** The most popular student living style, costing €350 to €600 per month depending on the city.
  - **Private Apartments:** Expensive, starting from €600+ per month.
- **Average Budget:** A student in a smaller German city (like Jena or Chemnitz) can live on €800-€900 per month, while major hubs like Munich, Frankfurt, or Hamburg require €1,100+ per month.

## Student Life, Integration, and Language

Adapting to German culture and social norms is essential for Nepali students.

- **The Semesterticket:** Included in your semester contribution, this card gives you free or heavily discounted unlimited travel on local public transport (buses, trams, regional trains) within your state [Source: DAAD — https://www.daad.de/en/study-and-research-in-germany/plan-your-studies/tuition-fees/].
- **German Language Integration:** While many master's programs are taught in English, communicating in German is crucial for daily tasks, renting apartments, and finding part-time work outside the university.
- **Student Unions:** The Studentenwerk offers counseling, psychological support, social events, and subsidized meals in university cafeterias (Mensa).
