# Studying in the USA — Work Rights and Student Life

Adjusting to student life in the US involves understanding strict employment regulations and adapting to a new academic and social culture. F-1 students have limited work rights, and compliance is critical to avoid deportation. As of June 2026, students must be aware of their legal boundaries [Source: US Immigration and Customs Enforcement — https://www.ice.gov].

## On-Campus Work Rights

- **Part-Time Hours:** F-1 students are permitted to work on-campus for up to 20 hours per week during regular academic semesters.
- **Full-Time Hours:** During official school breaks (summer and winter holidays), students can work on-campus full-time (up to 40 hours per week).
- **Types of Jobs:** Library assistant, dining hall worker, computer lab assistant, or administrative assistant. These jobs typically pay minimum wage ($7.25 to $15+ per hour, depending on the state).

## Off-Campus Work Limitations

- **Severe Restrictions:** Working off-campus (gas stations, restaurants, retail) without prior authorization is illegal and is a direct violation of F-1 visa status.
- **Severe Economic Hardship:** In rare cases, students can apply to USCIS for off-campus work authorization if they can prove unexpected, severe economic hardship beyond their control.

## Curricular Practical Training (CPT)

- **Definition:** CPT is temporary employment authorization for off-campus internships or cooperative education that are an integral part of the established curriculum [Source: Department of Homeland Security — https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov].
- **Eligibility:** Students must have completed one academic year of full-time study and get recommendation from their DSO.

## Student Life and Adaptation

- **Dormitory Living:** Most universities require first-year undergraduate students to live on-campus in residential halls and enroll in a meal plan.
- **Academic Integrity:** US universities have zero tolerance for plagiarism, cheating, or academic dishonesty. Violations can lead to suspension or expulsion.
- **Culture Shock:** Adapting to self-study, active classroom participation, and diverse food options. Support systems include international student advisor offices and student counseling centers.


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
