# Studying in the USA — Post-Study Work and Long-Term Options

Many Nepali students choose the US because of the opportunities to gain professional work experience after completing their degrees. Navigating post-study work authorization requires careful timing and compliance with immigration laws. As of June 2026, the US offers structured post-study work programs for qualified graduates [Source: US Citizenship and Immigration Services — https://www.uscis.gov].

## Optional Practical Training (OPT)

- **Definition:** OPT is temporary employment directly related to an F-1 student's major area of study.
- **12-Month Period:** Graduates are eligible for up to 12 months of OPT work authorization per degree level (Bachelor's, Master's, PhD).
- **Application Window:** Students must apply to USCIS within 90 days before their program end date and up to 60 days after. They must receive an Employment Authorization Document (EAD) card before starting work [Source: Department of Homeland Security — https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov].

## STEM OPT Extension

- **24-Month Extension:** Graduates with qualifying Science, Technology, Engineering, or Math (STEM) degrees can apply for a 24-month extension, allowing a total of 36 months (3 years) of post-study work rights.
- **E-Verify Employer:** To qualify, the employer must be enrolled in the US government's E-Verify program.

## H-1B Visa Transition

- **Definition:** The H-1B is a temporary specialty occupation visa that allows US employers to employ foreign workers in professional roles.
- **Lottery System:** Because demand exceeds the annual cap of 85,000 visas, H-1B visas are distributed via a random lottery. Employers must sponsor the graduate for the lottery.
- **Dual Intent:** Unlike F-1, the H-1B visa is a "dual-intent" visa, meaning holders can legally seek permanent residency (Green Card) while on this status.

## Returning to Nepal

A degree and professional work experience from the US are highly valued in Nepal. Returning graduates find leadership positions in multinational corporations, non-governmental organizations, banks, tech startups, or launch their own business ventures.


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
