# Studying in the USA — Institutions, Courses, and Intakes

The US higher education landscape is vast, featuring different types of institutions and flexible learning structures. Understanding these options helps Nepali students make informed choices that align with their career goals and financial capacity. As of June 2026, selecting the right institution type and intake is crucial for a successful application timeline [Source: EducationUSA — https://educationusa.state.gov].

## Types of Institutions

- **Public State Universities:** Funded by state governments, these are typically large institutions with tens of thousands of students. They offer lower tuition rates for state residents, while international students pay "out-of-state" tuition. Examples include the University of Texas system and the State University of New York (SUNY).
- **Private Universities:** Funded by tuition, endowments, and research grants. They generally have higher tuition fees than public universities, but they often have larger financial aid pools and offer substantial scholarships.
- **Liberal Arts Colleges (LACs):** Focus primarily on undergraduate education in the arts, humanities, social sciences, and natural sciences. They offer small class sizes, close interaction with professors, and generous need-based financial aid.
- **Community Colleges:** 2-year public institutions offering associate degrees and vocational certificates. They are highly affordable and have open-door admission policies.

## Popular Fields of Study

- **STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Math):** Computer Science, Data Science, Cybersecurity, Mechanical Engineering, and Biotechnology are highly sought after due to the 3-year OPT work rights [Source: Department of Homeland Security — https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov].
- **Business Administration:** Finance, Business Analytics, Management, and MBA programs are popular for their career outcomes.
- **Health Sciences:** Nursing, Public Health, and Pre-medical tracks.

## Main Intake Seasons

US universities operate primarily on a semester system, with three main intakes:

- **Fall Intake (August/September):** The primary intake for all universities. Offers the maximum number of courses, scholarships, and graduate assistantships. Deadlines range from November (Early Decision) to January-March (Regular Decision).
- **Spring Intake (January/February):** The secondary intake. Fewer courses and scholarships are available. Deadlines are typically between August and October of the previous year.
- **Summer Intake (May/June):** Limited to specific programs, ESL courses, or short-term certifications.

## Accreditation and SEVP Approval

Nepali students must ensure that the school they apply to is accredited by a recognized regional accrediting body and approved by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) to issue Form I-20 [Source: US Immigration and Customs Enforcement — https://www.ice.gov]. Applying to unaccredited or non-SEVP certified institutions will result in visa denial.
