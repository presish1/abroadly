# Studying in the USA — Application Timeline and Nepali Student FAQ

A successful application to a US university requires structured planning and timely execution. This guide provides a standard timeline and answers frequently asked questions by Nepali students. As of June 2026, the scheduling system and test deadlines require prompt preparation [Source: EducationUSA — https://educationusa.state.gov].

## Month-by-Month Application Timeline (For Fall Intake)

- **May - June (15 months before):** Research universities, understand requirements, and start preparation for tests (SAT, GRE, IELTS, TOEFL).
- **July - August:** Register for and take standardized tests. Draft the Statement of Purpose (SOP).
- **September - October:** Finalize your list of 6-10 universities. Request Letters of Recommendation (LORs) from teachers/professors. Start Common App.
- **November - December:** Submit applications for Early Action/Early Decision. Continue regular applications.
- **January - February:** Submit Regular Decision applications. Send mid-year grades if requested.
- **March - April:** Receive admission decisions and scholarship offers. Compare packages.
- **May:** Confirm acceptance, pay enrollment deposit, and submit bank balance certificates to request Form I-20 [Source: Department of Homeland Security — https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov].
- **June - July:** Pay SEVIS fee, complete DS-160, book appointments, and attend the visa interview at the US Embassy in Kathmandu [Source: US Embassy in Nepal — https://np.usembassy.gov].
- **August:** Attend pre-departure briefings, pack, and fly to the US.

## Frequently Asked Questions

- **Can I pay my tuition fees through part-time jobs?** No. On-campus jobs pay only enough for pocket money and food. You must have family sponsors or scholarships to cover tuition.
- **Can I sponsor myself?** Only if you have a documented business, employment history, and significant personal savings. Otherwise, parents or family members must sponsor you.
- **What is a SEVIS fee?** The Student and Exchange Visitor Information System fee paid to the US government to track F-1 students. It costs $350.


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
