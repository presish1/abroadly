# Studying in the USA — Official Sources

For accurate, reliable, and up-to-date information regarding studying in the United States, applications, and visa guidelines, students and counselors should refer exclusively to official government and institutional portals. As of June 2026, relying on official sources is vital due to frequent updates in international student policies [Source: US Department of State — https://travel.state.gov].

## Key Portals and Official Links

- **EducationUSA Nepal:** The official source for advising on US higher education.
  - Portal: https://educationusa.state.gov
- **US Department of State — Bureau of Consular Affairs:** The official portal for student visa (F-1) guidelines, application fees, and DS-160 instructions.
  - Portal: https://travel.state.gov
- **US Embassy in Nepal:** Provides local instructions for visa scheduling, security guidelines, and embassy location in Maharajgunj, Kathmandu.
  - Portal: https://np.usembassy.gov
- **Department of Homeland Security (DHS) — Study in the States:** Official portal for tracking student rules, SEVIS fee payments, and maintaining status.
  - Portal: https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov
- **US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) — SEVP:** Guidelines on work rights, CPT, and OPT regulations.
  - Portal: https://www.ice.gov
- **US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS):** Instructions on post-study employment, EAD cards, OPT filings, and H-1B work visas.
  - Portal: https://www.uscis.gov

## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
- **Question:** Are there language proficiency exemptions available?
  **Answer:** Some universities offer English proficiency waivers if the student's previous medium of instruction was entirely in English. However, taking tests like IELTS, TOEFL, or Duolingo is strongly recommended to strengthen the student visa application and demonstrate academic readiness.
- **Question:** What should a student do in case of a visa delay?
  **Answer:** If visa processing is delayed, students should immediately contact the university's international admission office to request an enrollment deferral or a late arrival letter. This ensures the student remains in status and does not violate immigration guidelines.
