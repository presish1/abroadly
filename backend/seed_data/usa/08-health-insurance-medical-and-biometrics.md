# Studying in the USA — Health Insurance, Medical, and Biometrics

International students in the United States must comply with mandatory health regulations and embassy procedures before and after arrival. Navigating these requirements is essential to maintain F-1 status and ensure personal well-being. As of June 2026, healthcare compliance is monitored closely by both US educational institutions and immigration services [Source: Department of Homeland Security — https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov].

## Student Health Insurance

Unlike many European countries, the US does not have a public health insurance system for international students.
- **Mandatory Enrollment:** Most universities require international students to enroll in the university's group health insurance plan. These plans cost between $1,500 and $4,000 per year.
- **Insurance Waiver:** If a university allows, students can purchase a cheaper, comprehensive policy from an approved third-party provider and apply for an insurance waiver. The policy must meet the university's strict coverage criteria.

## Immunization and Medical History

Before starting classes, students must submit a completed Health History and Immunization Form.
- **Required Vaccines:** Most universities mandate proof of vaccination for MMR (Measles, Mumps, Rubella), Meningitis, Varicella (Chickenpox), Tetanus-Diphtheria, and Hepatitis B.
- **Tuberculosis (TB) Testing:** Nepali students are often required to undergo TB testing (either a skin test or blood test) upon arrival or provide a negative chest X-ray report from Nepal.

## Biometrics and Security

In Nepal, the visa application process involves a biometrics appointment at the Visa Application Center (VAC), currently located at Chhaya Center in Thamel, Kathmandu.
- **Biometrics:** Fingerprints and digital photographs are taken.
- **Security Check:** Students must present their passport, DS-160 confirmation page, and appointment confirmation. No bags, mobile phones, or electronic devices are allowed inside the US Embassy during the interview [Source: US Embassy in Nepal — https://np.usembassy.gov].


## Additional Detailed RAG Reference and Q&A

This section provides supplementary reference material, detailed questions, and official guidelines to assist in search retrieval:

- **Question:** What is the primary official portal for updates?
  **Answer:** As of June 2026, students must monitor the official website to ensure all regulations, application procedures, and visa rules are followed. For official updates, refer to travel.state.gov for the USA and migri.fi for Finland. These sites are regularly updated with policy adjustments and fee schedules.
- **Question:** How do Nepali students apply for their NOC?
  **Answer:** Nepali students must apply online via the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MOEST) portal. The application requires a valid admission offer, proof of passport, and citizenship. The processing fee is NPR 2,000, and once approved, the NOC is required to exchange foreign currency and complete the visa process.
- **Question:** What is the role of sponsors in the application process?
  **Answer:** Sponsors provide the financial guarantee necessary to cover tuition fees, health insurance, and living expenses. In the USA, sponsors can be parents, relatives, or self-funded via bank loans. In Finland, sponsors must transfer funds to the student's personal bank account, as Migri does not accept third-party sponsor letters directly.
