# Studying in South Korea — Admission Documents and Nepal Preparation

Applying to South Korean institutions requires complete, legally verified documentation. The South Korean government has strict rules to prevent document fraud. For Nepali students, this means that academic and identity documents must undergo a detailed verification chain before they can be submitted for university admission and visa processing. As of June 2026, the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Nepal requires prior verification of all academic records by the Consular Section of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) in Nepal [Source: Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Nepal — https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/np-en/index.do].

---

## Academic Document Legalization (Consular Confirmation)

Because Nepal is not a member of the Apostille Convention, academic certificates must undergo the **Consular Confirmation** process. This is the most time-consuming part of the preparation:

1. **Notarization:** Make copies of the original certificates (SLC/SEE and Class 12, or Bachelor's transcripts and degrees) and have them notarized by a registered notary public in Nepal.
2. **Verification by Educational Boards:**
   * School-level documents must be verified by the National Examinations Board (NEB) or the Department of Education.
   * University-level documents must be verified by the respective university (e.g., Tribhuvan University Registrar’s office, Kathmandu University, etc.).
3. **Attestation by MoFA Nepal:** Take the verified documents to the Consular Section of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Nepal (Tripureshwor, Kathmandu) for official attestation.
4. **Embassy Confirmation:** Finally, submit the MoFA-attested documents to the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Kathmandu to receive the "Consular Confirmation" stamp. Without this stamp, Korean universities and immigration will reject the application [Source: Ministry of Education of the Republic of Korea — https://english.moe.go.kr/].

## Complete Document Checklist

Students must prepare the following documents for admission:

* **Legalized Academic Certificates:** Consularly confirmed transcripts, character certificates, and provisional/degree certificates.
* **Study Plan and Statement of Purpose:** A detailed letter written by the student explaining their academic goals, reasons for choosing South Korea, and future plans. For Korean-medium courses, this must be written in Korean; for English-medium courses, in English.
* **Language Test Scores:** Original TOPIK score reports (for Korean programs) or official IELTS/TOEFL certificates (for English programs).
* **Identity Documents:**
   * Valid Passport (with at least 1–2 years of validity remaining).
   * Applicant’s and parents' Citizenship Certificates (must be translated into English and notarized).
   * Birth Certificate (translated and notarized).
   * **Relationship Certificate:** A municipal document proving the connection between the student and the financial sponsor (typically parents) [Source: Korea Visa Portal — https://www.visa.go.kr/].

## No Objection Certificate (NOC) from Nepal Government

Under Nepalese law, any student going abroad for studies must obtain a **No Objection Certificate (NOC)** from the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MoEST) of Nepal.

* **Usage:** The NOC is required by commercial banks in Nepal to execute outward telegraphic transfers (TT) to pay the Korean university tuition fees. It is also inspected by Nepalese immigration officers at Tribhuvan International Airport (TIA) before departure.
* **Application:** Completed online via the MoEST NOC portal, uploading the university's Letter of Admission, academic transcripts, and citizenship.
* **Collection:** Upon online approval, the student pays a fee of NPR 2,000 and collects the physical certificate from the MoEST office in Kathmandu [Source: Ministry of Education, Science and Technology of Nepal — https://moest.gov.np/].
