# Studying in South Korea — Work Rights and Student Life

Adapting to life in South Korea requires managing time effectively between study and part-time employment. The South Korean government permits international students to work part-time, but regulations are strictly enforced. As of June 2026, immigration authorities have tied work rights directly to academic performance, attendance, and Korean language proficiency (TOPIK scores) to ensure students prioritize their education [Source: Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Korea — https://www.moj.go.kr/EN/].

---

## Part-Time Work Rights (Siganje Arubaito)

International students on student visas cannot work immediately without permission. They must secure a **Part-Time Work Permit** from the local Immigration Office:

* **Wait Period for Language Students (D-4-1):** Language students must reside in Korea for at least **6 months** (two terms) before they can apply for a work permit. They must maintain an attendance record of **90% or higher** during those 6 months.
* **Regular Students (D-2):** Degree students can apply for a part-time work permit immediately upon arrival, provided their GPA is satisfactory (usually above 2.0/4.5).
* **Application Process:** The student must get recommendation signatures from their university's international advisor, fill out the application form, and submit it to immigration along with their ARC and contract details [Source: Korea Visa Portal — https://www.visa.go.kr/].

---

## TOPIK-Linked Hours and Restrictions

To encourage students to learn Korean, the maximum allowed work hours depend on the student's TOPIK level:

### 1. Language School Students (D-4-1)
* **With TOPIK Level 2 or higher:** Up to 20 hours per week during semesters.
* **Without TOPIK Level 2:** Restricted to a maximum of 10 hours per week.

### 2. Undergraduate Students (D-2-1 / D-2-2)
* **With TOPIK Level 3 or higher (Level 4 for seniors):** Up to 20 to 25 hours per week during semesters.
* **Without TOPIK Level 3:** Restricted to a maximum of 10 hours per week.
* **Vacations:** Unlimited work hours during official summer and winter vacations, provided they have the required TOPIK level.

### 3. Graduate Students (D-2-3 / D-2-4)
* **With TOPIK Level 4 or higher:** Up to 30 hours per week during semesters.
* **Without TOPIK Level 4:** Restricted to a maximum of 15 hours per week.

---

## Prohibited Sectors and Penalties

South Korea maintains absolute prohibitions on certain job sectors for students:

* **Strict Prohibitions:** Students are **not allowed** to work in manufacturing (factories), construction sites, or private tutoring (English/math academies or home tutoring).
* **Consequences:** Working in a factory or construction site without special authorization is a major immigration offense. If caught, both the student and the employer face heavy fines, and the student's visa will be cancelled, leading to deportation.
* **Permitted Jobs:** Service industries (restaurants, cafes, convenience stores like CU/GS25), university library duties, or research assistantships.
* **Wages:** Hourly wages are governed by the national minimum wage. The minimum wage is **KRW 10,030 per hour** [Source: Ministry of Employment and Labor of South Korea — https://www.moel.go.kr/english/].

## Student Life and Integration

* **Banking:** Setting up an account at major banks like Hana Bank, Woori Bank, or KB Kookmin Bank is simple after obtaining the ARC. These banks offer English and translation services.
* **Housing and Dining:** Most students buy meals at university cafeterias (Haksik) for KRW 4,500 to KRW 6,000, which is highly economical.
* **University Clubs (Dongari):** Joining university clubs is the best way for Nepali students to integrate, practice Korean, and make local friends.
