# Studying in South Korea — Health Insurance, Medical, and Biometrics

International students in South Korea must comply with national policies on public health, medical insurance, and immigration registration. The South Korean government has integrated these systems to monitor the stay of foreign nationals and ensure they have access to quality healthcare. As of June 2026, health insurance enrollment is automatic, and biometrics remain a key requirement for securing residency in South Korea [Source: Ministry of Health and Welfare of South Korea — https://www.mohw.go.kr/eng/].

---

## National Health Insurance (NHI) of Korea

South Korea operates a mandatory national healthcare system that covers all international students on D-2 and D-4 visas:

* **Automatic Enrollment:** International students are automatically registered in the National Health Insurance (NHI) system upon completing their foreign registration (obtaining their Alien Registration Card) and registering their local address.
* **Premium Cost:** The monthly premium for international students is heavily discounted compared to the national average. The standard premium is approximately **KRW 70,000 to KRW 80,000 per month** (approx. NPR 7,000 to NPR 8,000). Failing to pay the monthly premiums will result in visa extension denials and restrictions on accessing medical treatment.
* **Benefits:** The NHI covers **70% to 80% of dental, clinical, and hospitalisation costs** for accidents, illnesses, and prescription medication. Specialized cosmetic surgeries and certain high-end procedures are excluded.
* **Private Insurance:** Some students purchase cheap private health insurance in Nepal or Korea for their first few weeks before their ARC and NHI are finalized, but this does not excuse them from the mandatory NHI enrollment [Source: National Institute for International Education — https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/].

## Medical Examination and Tuberculosis Certification

South Korea enforces strict quarantine and disease prevention measures:

* **Pre-Departure Tuberculosis (TB) Screening:** Because Nepal is on South Korea's list of high-risk countries for tuberculosis, all student visa applicants at the Embassy of South Korea in Nepal must submit a **Tuberculosis (TB) Health Certificate**.
* **Designated Hospitals:** The certificate must be issued by one of the embassy-approved medical centers in Kathmandu (e.g., Patan Hospital, Shukraraj Tropical Hospital, Civil Service Hospital). A chest X-ray is the primary screening tool.
* **On-Arrival University Health Check:** Universities require all newly arrived students to undergo a basic physical check-up (blood tests, urine analysis, and chest X-rays) at the university medical center or a local public health office.

## Biometrics and the Alien Registration Card (ARC)

The Alien Registration Card (ARC), officially renamed the **Residence Card**, is the essential identity document for long-term foreign residents:

* **Airport Biometrics:** Upon arrival at South Korean airports (such as Incheon or Gimhae), immigration officers scan the student's fingerprints and capture a facial image.
* **Applying for the ARC:** Any student staying in South Korea for more than 90 days must apply for their ARC at their regional Immigration Office within 90 days of arrival.
* **Required Documents:** University registration certificate, proof of residence (dormitory certificate or lease agreement), passport, application fee (approx. KRW 30,000), and a passport photo.
* **Important Address Rule:** If a student moves to a new apartment, they must report their new address to the local district office (Gu-office) or immigration office within **14 days**. Failure to do so leads to heavy fines under the Immigration Act [Source: Korea Visa Portal — https://www.visa.go.kr/].
