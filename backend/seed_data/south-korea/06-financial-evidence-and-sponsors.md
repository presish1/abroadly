# Studying in South Korea — Financial Evidence and Sponsors

To prevent international students from facing financial hardship and illegal work situations, South Korean immigration and universities require strict proof of financial capability. Applicants must show that they or their immediate family sponsors possess sufficient liquid assets to cover tuition and living costs. As of June 2026, the financial thresholds are set in USD and are monitored closely during both the university admission phase and the visa stamp phase at the embassy [Source: Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Korea — https://www.moj.go.kr/EN/].

---

## Visa-Specific Bank Balance Requirements

The minimum bank balance required depends on the type of visa the student is applying for:

### 1. Regular Student Visa (D-2)
* **Target Programs:** Bachelor's, Associate, Master's, or PhD programs.
* **Minimum Balance:** **USD 20,000** (or the equivalent in NPR, approximately NPR 2.7 million).
* **Location of Funds:** If applying to a university in the Seoul Metropolitan Area, the USD 20,000 threshold is strictly enforced. For some provincial universities, the requirement may be slightly lower (around USD 16,000 to USD 18,000), but USD 20,000 remains the safe standard.

### 2. General Student Visa (D-4-1)
* **Target Programs:** Korean Language Program at university language centers.
* **Minimum Balance:** **USD 10,000** (or the equivalent in NPR, approximately NPR 1.35 million) [Source: Korea Visa Portal — https://www.visa.go.kr/].

---

## Key Rules for Bank Proofs

* **Approved Institutions:** The bank balance certificate and the preceding transaction statements must be issued by a recognized commercial bank (Class 'A' bank in Nepal). Finance companies and co-operative banks are not accepted.
* **Holding Period:** The funds must be deposited and held in the account for a specific period. Many Korean universities require the funds to be frozen for 1 to 3 months, and the balance certificate must be issued within 30 days prior to the visa submission.
* **Account Ownership:** The bank account must be under the name of the student or their primary financial sponsor (parents or spouse). If the account belongs to a sponsor, a legalized relationship certificate is mandatory.

---

## Sponsor Income and Verification

In addition to the static bank balance, the sponsor must show a continuous, stable flow of income to explain how the bank funds were accumulated.

* **Eligible Sponsors:** Only immediate family members (father, mother, spouse, or siblings) are recognized as valid sponsors. Sponsorship from uncles, aunts, or distant relatives is usually rejected by the embassy.
* **Minimum Annual Income:** The sponsor's total annual income should be at least **NPR 800,000 to NPR 1,200,000** from verifiable sources.
* **Required Documentation:**
  * **Salary Earners:** A letter of employment showing designation, monthly salary, and joining date, along with the sponsor's bank statement showing salary deposits.
  * **Business Owners:** Company registration certificate, tax registration (PAN/VAT), audited financial statements, and company tax clearance certificates for the last 3 fiscal years.
  * **Agriculture and Land Income:** Land ownership certificates (*Lalpurja*), municipality letters verifying agricultural income, and local tax receipts [Source: Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Nepal — https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/np-en/index.do].
* **Tax Clearance:** Personal tax clearance certificates from the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Nepal are required for the sponsor's primary income sources.
