# Studying in South Korea — Post-Study Work and Long-Term Options

Completing higher education in South Korea opens up multiple pathways for Nepali students to enter the local job market and transition to long-term residency status. South Korea has established specific visa categories for international graduates to encourage talent retention. As of June 2026, the Ministry of Justice has revised the points systems and regional residency requirements to steer international graduates toward regional economic hubs [Source: Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Korea — https://www.moj.go.kr/EN/].

---

## The Job Seeking Visa (D-10)

Graduates of South Korean universities who have not yet secured employment can apply for the **D-10 Job Seeking Visa**:

* **Duration:** Issued for 6 months, and can be extended up to a maximum of 2 years (typically 1 year for junior college graduates and 2 years for bachelor's or graduate degree holders).
* **Points System:** Applicants must score at least **60 points** out of a total of 190 points on the D-10 points-based table. Points are awarded based on:
  * **Age:** Highest points for ages 20 to 29.
  * **Education:** Points based on the degree level (Associate, Bachelor's, Master's, PhD) and whether it was obtained in Korea.
  * **GPA:** GPA of 3.0/4.5 or higher.
  * **Language:** TOPIK Level 3 or higher.
  * **Prior Study/Work:** Duration of stay in Korea and internship experience.
* **Limitations:** The D-10 visa allows internships and job hunting but does not permit full-time regular employment [Source: Korea Visa Portal — https://www.visa.go.kr/].

## Professional Work Visa: E-7 Visa

Once a graduate secures a professional job offer matching their field of study, they transition to the **E-7 Foreign National of Ability Visa**:

* **Major Alignment:** The job must match the graduate's major. For example, a computer science graduate can transition to an E-7 visa as a software developer, but not as a general retail manager.
* **GNI Salary Requirement:** The employer must pay a salary that meets the legal minimum, which is strictly set at **80% of the previous year's Gross National Income (GNI) per capita** of South Korea. For corporate IT and business roles, this threshold is approximately KRW 34,000,000 to KRW 36,000,000 per year. Employers paying below this rate will have the visa application rejected.
* **Sponsorship:** The employer must sponsor the visa, proving they meet the local-to-foreign employee ratios (usually at least 5 Korean employees for every 1 foreign employee).

## Residency and Regional Visas (F-2-R and F-2-7)

For graduates wishing to establish permanent roots in South Korea, several long-term visas are available:

### 1. Regional Specialized Residency Visa (F-2-R)
* **Goal:** To address population decline in provincial areas.
* **Path:** Graduates who agree to live and work in designated regional municipalities (outside Seoul and parts of Gyeonggi) for at least **5 years** can obtain the F-2-R residency visa.
* **Benefit:** Offers high flexibility in changing employers within the region and allows spouses to work.

### 2. Points-Based Resident Visa (F-2-7)
* **Goal:** A coveted visa for highly skilled professionals.
* **Path:** Evaluates applicants on a points chart (scoring based on age, education, annual salary, TOPIK level, and completion of the Korea Immigration and Integration Program - KIIP).
* **Benefit:** Grants residency status and allows the holder to work in almost any field in Korea without employer sponsorship [Source: Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Korea — https://www.moj.go.kr/EN/].
