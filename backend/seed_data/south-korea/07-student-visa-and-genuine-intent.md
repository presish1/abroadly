# Studying in South Korea — Student Visa and Genuine Intent

Applying for a student visa to South Korea is the final gateway to starting an academic journey. The South Korean government categorizes student visas based on the type of program. The evaluation process is stringent, designed to verify the applicant's academic motivation and rule out immigration for illegal employment. As of June 2026, the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Kathmandu conducts thorough interviews and document checks to confirm "genuine student intent" [Source: Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Nepal — https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/np-en/index.do].

---

## Student Visa Classifications

South Korea issues two primary student visa categories:

### 1. General Student Visa (D-4-1)
* **Purpose:** For students enrolled in Korean language training programs at university-affiliated language institutes.
* **Validity:** Typically issued for 6 months, renewable in Korea based on attendance and academic progress.
* **Limitations:** Students cannot work for the first 6 months.

### 2. Regular Student Visa (D-2)
* **Purpose:** For students enrolled in full-time degree programs. Subcategories include:
  * **D-2-1:** Associate Degree (Junior College)
  * **D-2-2:** Bachelor’s Degree (Undergraduate)
  * **D-2-3:** Master’s Degree
  * **D-2-4:** Doctoral Degree (PhD)
  * **D-2-5:** Research Study [Source: Korea Visa Portal — https://www.visa.go.kr/].

---

## Step-by-Step Visa Application Process

Once a student is admitted and has paid their tuition fees, they follow these steps to secure their visa:

1. **Receive the Certificate of Admission (CoA):** The South Korean university sends the original physical CoA (or a digital copy for certain accredited universities) along with the Business Registration Certificate of the university.
2. **Book a Visa Appointment:** Submit the visa application form and schedule an appointment at the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Nepal.
3. **Submit the Visa Package:** The application package must include:
   * Original Certificate of Admission (CoA).
   * Completed visa application form and photographs.
   * Legally confirmed academic records.
   * Bank balance certificate and sponsor’s financial proofs.
   * Tuberculosis (TB) Health Certificate from an embassy-designated hospital.
4. **Embassy Interview:** The visa officer may conduct an interview in Korean or English depending on the program's language.
5. **Passport Collection:** The typical visa processing time is 7 to 15 working days.

## Demonstrating Genuine Student Intent

To pass the embassy assessment, students must show they are focused on education:

### 1. Program and University Knowledge
Students must be able to speak details about their university, including:
* The name and geographical location of the university.
* The specific major they will study and key subjects in the curriculum.
* The names of professors or research labs (especially for graduate students).
* Why they chose South Korea over other study abroad options.

### 2. Language Preparation
For D-2 applicants, conversational fluency in either English (for English-medium) or Korean (for Korean-medium) is tested. For D-4-1 applicants, demonstrating that they have already studied basic Korean (knowing the alphabet Hangul and basic self-introduction) shows genuine interest and preparation.

### 3. Clear Post-Study Intentions
Students must explain their career goals after graduation. Stating a clear plan to return to Nepal to contribute to local industries, or to legally transition to a professional work visa (such as the E-7 or D-10) in South Korea, is critical. Any suggestion of staying illegally or working beyond the permitted hours will lead to visa rejection [Source: Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Korea — https://www.moj.go.kr/EN/].
